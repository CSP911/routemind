# How a garnishment deduction reads on the payslip

A garnishment deduction appears as its own line on the deduction side, labelled with the court case reference number rather than the creditor's name. It sits below the four insurance lines and income tax, not mixed in with them.

## What it doesn't touch

It never replaces or reduces the insurance or tax lines above it — those are calculated first, in full, and the garnishment is worked out on what's left.

**A garnishment deduction is calculated on net pay after insurance and tax, not on gross pay.** Running it before those lines would understate the protected amount and could push a low earner below the floor the law sets for them.

If the order is an assignment rather than a seizure, the line keeps appearing every month at the same logic, for as long as the order stays open — it does not get folded into a single lump because the debt has a known total.

The other deduction lines it sits beside — pension, health, long-term care, employment insurance, tax — are recalculated on their own schedules, not affected by this one.
