# A reimbursement claim with no receipt attached

An employee submits a 45,000 KRW taxi claim with a note explaining the receipt was lost, rather than a completed form returned for correction.

## What payroll does

The claim is not rejected outright the way an incomplete form is. Payroll pays the 45,000 KRW, but **as a taxable payment line, not a tax-free reimbursement** — without the receipt there is no way to certify it as a genuine repaid cost rather than income, so it is added to gross pay, taxed, and carries the usual insurance deductions with it.

## Why this differs from the standing allowance

A standing allowance's condition is checked once and the monthly figure then runs untouched even without fresh paperwork each month. A reimbursement has no such standing status — each claim is judged on its own evidence, and a gap in that evidence changes the tax outcome, not the willingness to pay.

The reverse situation — a cost with no cap at all once it is properly evidenced — is worth setting out too.
