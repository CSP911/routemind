# Severance pay calculation

Severance pay is calculated from average wage, not basic pay, and from total days of continuous service, not years worked.

## The formula

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Average wage | Total wages paid in the last 3 months before leaving, divided by the number of calendar days in that period |
| Severance amount | Average daily wage × 30 days × (total service days ÷ 365) |

"Wages paid" for this calculation includes basic pay and regular allowances actually paid in the 3-month window; it excludes irregular bonuses that don't recur on a fixed schedule.

## Why the last 3 months matter

Because the base is a 3-month average, a spike or a dip in pay right before leaving — a large one-off allowance, or a period of unpaid leave — moves the whole severance figure, not just that month's pay.

**A promotion or pay rise landing inside the final 3 months pulls the whole average wage up, not just the new-rate months.** Timing a raise around a departure date changes the severance payout, and payroll calculates on whatever the last 3 months actually show.

The DB and DC pension types apply this same average-wage base differently — DB pays it directly, DC turns it into a running monthly contribution.
