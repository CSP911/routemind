# Unused leave payout

Annual leave that goes unused doesn't just disappear. It converts to money in one of two situations: the employee leaves the company with days still on the books, or the company runs a use-it-or-lose-it policy and settles unused days once the leave year closes.

## When it's triggered

- **Leaving employment** — remaining unused days are costed out and added to the final payslip.
- **Annual leave settlement** — for employees still on headcount, unused days from the closed leave year are paid out on the payslip following the settlement date, and the leave balance resets to zero.

## How it's taxed

Leave payout runs through ordinary payroll withholding — the same simplified tax table applied to a regular month's salary — and shows up as a normal payment line.

**Unused leave payout does not run through the severance calculation or its retirement-income tax schedule.** It is ordinary payroll income from the day it's calculated to the day it's taxed, even when it's paid on the same final payslip as everything else a leaver receives.

What it does not share with severance is the wage figure it converts on — that's the next question.
