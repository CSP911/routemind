# Signing bonus clawback conditions

## The structure
A signing bonus is paid upfront — usually with the first or second payslip — against a minimum service period stated in the offer letter, commonly 12 or 24 months. Leaving before that period ends triggers a clawback of some or all of the bonus.

## How the amount is set
Most schemes scale the clawback down as service time passes: an employee who leaves halfway through a 24-month condition typically owes half the bonus, not the full amount. The offer letter, not payroll, sets the exact schedule.

| Time served against condition | Typical clawback owed |
|---|---|
| 0–25% | Full amount |
| 25–50% | 75% |
| 50–75% | 50% |
| 75–100% | 25% |
| Full period served | None |

**The clawback is calculated on the gross bonus amount originally granted, not on what the employee actually received after tax.** Because tax and insurance were already withheld from the bonus when it was paid, recovering the full gross figure from final pay is not the same as reversing the net payment — see the recovery note for how payroll actually collects it.
