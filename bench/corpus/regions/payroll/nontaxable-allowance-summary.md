# Each allowance, its cap and its condition, side by side

The four capped, tax-free allowances share the same 200,000 KRW monthly cap but fail differently when their condition isn't met.

## The comparison

| Allowance | Cap | Condition | If the condition fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meals | 200,000 KRW | Not paid alongside meals in kind | Whole line becomes taxable pay |
| Own-car | 200,000 KRW | Own vehicle, used for work; not with a mileage claim | Whole line becomes taxable pay |
| Childcare | 200,000 KRW | Child aged 6 or under | Whole line becomes taxable pay |
| Research | 200,000 KRW | Qualifying role only | Whole line becomes taxable pay |

**None of these four fail the way a cap failure does.** Going over the 200,000 KRW cap on any of them only taxes the excess — the allowance survives up to the cap. Failing the condition is different: it doesn't touch the cap at all, and taxes the entire line, cap or no cap, for every month the condition doesn't hold.

## Reading this table

Use it to spot which trap is in front of you: a number problem is a cap problem and is partial; a status problem — age, role, ownership, a duplicate claim — is a condition problem and is total.

How the taxable portion of any of these lines actually splits out on the payslip is covered separately.
