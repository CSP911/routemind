# Retirement pension types compared

Every employee's severance sits under one of two pension structures, chosen by the company and applied to that employee, not switched at will.

## Comparison

| | DB (defined benefit) | DC (defined contribution) |
|---|---|---|
| What's fixed | The payout formula | The monthly contribution |
| Who bears investment risk | Company | Employee |
| Payout amount | Exactly the statutory formula on leaving | Whatever the account has grown to |
| When contributions move | N/A — company funds the obligation as needed | Paid into the employee's account at least annually |

## Why it matters to an employee

Under DB, the leaving-day calculation (average wage × service days) is the final answer. Under DC, that same formula only sets what the company must contribute each period into the account — the account's actual investment return then decides the final balance.

**A DC account's payout is not guaranteed to match the DB formula amount at leaving — it can be higher or lower depending on how the contributions were invested.** Comparing the two types on the assumption they land on the same number is the trap.
