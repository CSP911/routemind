# Paying someone who isn't an employee

A contractor is paid against an invoice for a defined piece of work, not a monthly salary for ongoing service. They don't join a payslip run, don't get enrolled in the four insurances, and don't accrue leave or severance. Payroll's only job is to withhold the correct tax from the invoice amount and pay the net figure — the withholding rate depends on where the contractor is based.

## Two payment routes

A contractor resident in Korea is paid under the freelancer withholding rate. A contractor based overseas is paid under a different, higher default rate unless a tax treaty applies. Both routes need the contractor's registration details before the first payment can go out.

## What payroll does not manage

No basic pay, no allowances, no insurance lines, no year-end settlement. A contractor who wants a tax refund or owes more tax settles it themselves through their own annual filing, not through payroll's February run.

## The trap

**A contractor paid the same fixed amount every month, on a fixed schedule, looking a lot like a salary, is not automatically an employee — but working fixed hours under direct supervision with company equipment usually is.** The invoice format doesn't decide the classification; the actual working pattern does.
