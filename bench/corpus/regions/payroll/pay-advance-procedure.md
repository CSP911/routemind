# Salary advance request and repayment

An advance is a draw against pay not yet earned — the employee receives money now, and payroll recovers it from a later payslip. It needs a signed-off request before payment, same as a draft rise or an off-cycle payment.

## How much can be advanced

| Service length | Maximum advance | Repayment |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1 year | Up to 1 month's basic pay | Single deduction, next payslip |
| 1 year or more | Up to 2 months' basic pay | Split across up to 3 payslips |

## Tax at each end

The advance is paid out and taxed as ordinary income in the month it's disbursed — withholding runs against it exactly as it would against a regular payslip. **Repayment is a plain deduction line on the later payslip, not a reversal of that withholding.** It reduces net pay in the repayment month; it does not reopen or refund the tax already withheld when the advance was paid.

An employee repaying across three payslips sees three deduction lines, not three separate tax events — the tax was settled once, at disbursement.

Where the amount involved is a bonus rather than an advance against ordinary pay, a different withholding method applies entirely.
