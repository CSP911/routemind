# Bonus types and how they're paid

Payroll handles three kinds of bonus, and each is set up differently on the system.

## Regular bonus
Paid on a fixed schedule set in the employment contract — commonly a set month, or split across two payments a year. Treated as ordinary earned income for tax and insurance, same as basic pay.

## Performance bonus
Paid once a year after results are confirmed, on a date HR sets rather than the contract. The amount is discretionary and can be zero.

## Signing bonus
Paid once, usually with the first or second payslip after joining, conditional on staying a minimum period. See the clawback notes if that condition isn't met.

| Bonus type | Timing | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | Fixed date(s) in contract | Fixed or formula-based |
| Performance | Set annually by HR | Discretionary |
| Signing | On or shortly after joining | Fixed, conditional |

**A bonus is never a nontaxable line.** All three types are taxed and carry insurance deductions exactly as basic pay does — there is no allowance-style cap that shelters any part of it.
