# When a contractor looks like an employee

Freelancer withholding assumes an independent contractor: someone who sets their own hours, uses their own equipment, and can take on other clients. When the actual arrangement drifts away from that, the classification is at risk regardless of what the invoice says.

## What tips it over

- Fixed working hours set by the company, not the contractor
- Direct day-to-day supervision, not a defined deliverable
- Company-provided equipment or workspace
- No ability to take other clients during the engagement

None of these alone is decisive; a fixed monthly amount paid on a regular schedule is common for genuine contractors too and doesn't by itself change anything. It's the combination — schedule plus supervision plus exclusivity — that a labour inspector or the National Pension Service looks for.

## What reclassification costs

**If a contractor is reclassified as an employee, the correction is retroactive** — payroll owes the four insurances back-dated to the actual start date, and the 3.3% freelancer withholding already paid has to be reconciled against what payroll withholding would have taken across the same months. The exposure is the company's, not the contractor's.
