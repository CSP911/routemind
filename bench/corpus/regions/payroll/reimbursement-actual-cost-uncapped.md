# Why a reimbursement has no cap

## The logic of actual cost

The 200,000 KRW caps on meals, own-car use, childcare and research exist because those are flat allowances paid whether or not the money was actually spent that way. A reimbursement repays a real, receipted cost, so there is no equivalent monthly ceiling — a 500,000 KRW client dinner, properly evidenced and approved, is repaid in full and tax free.

## Where a limit still shows up

**"No cap" does not mean "no check."** Travel reimbursement is compared against the policy's daily rate (20,000 KRW domestic, USD 50–80 overseas) as a benchmark of what's reasonable for the trip; a claim far above that benchmark gets queried for business justification even though there is no fixed number it is measured against, unlike the flat allowance table.

An employee expecting the same 200,000 KRW ceiling to apply here is working from the wrong table — that number belongs to the standing allowance, not to actual-cost repayment.
