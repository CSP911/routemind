# Recovering a signing bonus clawback

## Where it's deducted from
Payroll recovers the clawback from the employee's final pay wherever possible — final salary, unused leave payout, and any other amount still owed. If the clawback exceeds what's left to pay out, the shortfall is invoiced to the employee directly; payroll cannot deduct a leaver's severance pay to cover it, since severance is calculated and paid separately from the final payslip.

## The tax problem
Income tax and insurance were already withheld and remitted on the original gross bonus. Repaying the gross amount to the company does not automatically get that tax back — the employee's original withholding stands as filed unless payroll amends it.

## What payroll actually does
1. Confirm the clawback amount against the schedule in the offer letter.
2. Deduct what it can from final pay, itemised as a separate negative line, not netted silently into another figure.
3. Where the deduction exceeds final pay, raise a direct invoice for the balance.
4. File an amendment for the original bonus's withholding so the employee isn't left having paid tax on income they no longer keep.

**Repaying the clawback and correcting the tax withheld are two separate actions.** Settling the cash amount with the company does not by itself fix the tax record — without step 4, the employee has paid income tax on money they've since given back.

How an amendment like this is filed once the normal payslip correction has no month left to land on is covered in the leaver correction note.
