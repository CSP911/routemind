# Social insurance when someone holds a second job

An employee with two qualifying jobs is enrolled in national pension and health insurance separately at each employer. Each employer calculates its own contribution — 4.5% pension, 3.545% health — on what it pays, independent of what the other employer pays.

## What runs where

| Insurance | Basis with a second job |
|---|---|
| National pension | Per employer, on that employer's pay only |
| Health insurance | Per employer, on that employer's pay only |
| Employment insurance | Filed by the main job only |
| Industrial accident | Per employer, automatic |

## The trap

**Having a second job does not split or reduce the contribution on the main job's pay.** Each employer's 4.5% and 3.545% are calculated exactly as if the other job didn't exist. The one line that behaves differently is employment insurance, which is enrolled through whichever job is designated the main one — the second employer does not file it at all.
