# Severance pay

Every employee who completes at least one year of continuous service is owed a severance payment when they leave, calculated on average wage and length of service rather than on any payslip line. It sits under its own settlement, separate from the final month's pay.

## The two forms it can take

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| DB (defined benefit) | Company holds the obligation and pays out the statutory formula amount directly on leaving |
| DC (defined contribution) | Company pays a monthly contribution into the employee's own pension account; the payout is whatever that account has grown to |

Which type applies is set at company or individual level, not chosen at the point of leaving.

## When it's paid

Settlement is due within 14 days of the last working day, extendable only by written agreement between employer and employee.

**Severance pay is not a line on the leaving payslip.** It is calculated and paid separately under its own settlement — looking for it on the final payslip is the wrong place to check; the leaving payslip closes out basic pay, allowances and the usual deductions only.
