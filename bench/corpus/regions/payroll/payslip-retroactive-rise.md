# A retroactive pay rise on a payslip

## When it happens

A rise approved after the fact — a March decision made effective from January, for example — is paid as a lump back-pay line once approved, not spread across the months it was earned.

## How it's shown

The back-pay amount appears as a separate line from current basic pay, labelled as a retroactive adjustment, in the month it is actually paid out.

## The trap

**The back-pay lump sum is taxed in the month it is paid, at that month's full amount — not divided across the months it covers.** A large lump sum can push that single month into a higher withholding bracket than any of the individual months it was earned in would have triggered on their own. This is a timing effect on withholding only; the year-end settlement reconciles the total tax owed for the year regardless of which month it was withheld in.

Corrections that are not retroactive rises follow a different, smaller mechanism, covered next.
