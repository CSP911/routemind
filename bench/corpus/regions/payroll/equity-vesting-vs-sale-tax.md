# Vesting tax versus sale tax

## Two separate tax events
Payroll withholds income tax once, at vesting or exercise, on the value described under tax withholding. Any later sale of the shares is a capital gain or loss, reported and taxed outside payroll entirely — through the employee's own securities account and, where required, their own tax filing.

| Event | Who taxes it | When |
|---|---|---|
| Vesting / exercise | Payroll, as earned income | Month of vesting or exercise |
| Sale of the shares | Not payroll — capital gains, employee's own filing | Whenever the shares are sold |

## The trap
**A falling share price after vesting does not refund the tax already withheld.** An employee taxed on a vesting-date value of 10,000,000 KRW who sells six months later at 6,000,000 KRW still owes the tax withheld on the original 10,000,000 KRW — payroll has no mechanism to adjust withholding for a loss that happens after the taxable event has already occurred.

This is also why the figure on the payslip in the vesting month and the amount an employee eventually receives from selling the shares are never expected to match.
