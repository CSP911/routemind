# Severance pay at a glance

A single reference for the four things severance pay determines: how much, where it sits, when it lands, and how it's taxed.

## The essentials

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Calculation | Average wage over the last 3 months × 30 days × years of service |
| Pension type | DB — fixed by the formula, company carries the investment risk — or DC — paid into the employee's own account, contributions fixed, account performance is the employee's risk |
| Payment window | Within 14 days of the last working day |
| Tax | Retirement income tax, computed on its own schedule |

## The trap

**Choosing DC does not make the money arrive any faster than DB.** The 14-day payment window applies to both pension types alike. What DC changes is where the money has been sitting and who absorbed the investment gain or loss up to that point — not the settlement deadline itself.

## Everything else

The formula's exact wage definition, the pension types' mechanics, the extension rule on the 14-day window, and how retirement income tax is actually computed each have their own detail — this page only exists so a leaver's severance figure can be sanity-checked in one glance without opening four separate documents. The leaving payslip itself carries none of this; it settles on its own schedule, apart from everything else on that final payslip.
