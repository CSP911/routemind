# Pay changes that need a decision

Most pay changes follow a fixed calendar: the annual review, the July insurance reset, the standard payslip cut-off. Three kinds of change don't — payroll needs someone to actively decide before any figure moves.

## The three kinds

- **A rise still in draft.** A pay review recommendation can sit approved-in-draft in the review system for weeks before it's formally signed off. Draft status is not a decision.
- **An off-cycle payment.** A one-time payment outside the monthly run — a relocation payment, an urgent correction — needs its own approval and its own run.
- **An advance.** A request to draw pay before it's earned, repaid out of a later payslip.

## What counts as a decision

Payroll does not act on any of the three from a request, a draft, or a verbal approval. Each needs a signed-off instruction naming the amount, the payslip it lands on, and — for an advance — the repayment schedule.

**A draft figure approved in the review system is not the same as a signed-off instruction to pay it.** Until the sign-off step runs, payroll treats the draft as provisional and continues paying the old figure.

The mechanics of each — how the rise lands, how the off-cycle run is taxed, how an advance is recovered — are covered separately.
