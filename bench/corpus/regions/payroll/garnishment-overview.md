# Garnishment and deductions by order

A garnishment order (압류명령) reaches payroll from a court, the National Tax Service, or the National Health Insurance Service, to recover a debt, unpaid tax, or unpaid support. It arrives as a document naming the employee, the creditor, and the amount owed — it is not a request the employee can decline.

## What it attaches

An order attaches one of two things: the wage itself (a seizure or collection order, often capped at a stated total), or the right to receive it (an assignment order, which keeps redirecting payments to the creditor until the debt clears). Both show up on payroll the same way — as a new deduction line.

## Payroll's role

Payroll does not judge the underlying debt or negotiate with the creditor. It verifies the order, works out the protected portion, deducts the remainder, and pays it to the named account — never to the employee.

**A garnishment order does not require the employee's consent, and it applies from the first payslip payroll can still act on, not from some later notice date.**

What it can never reach is fixed by wage band, not by how much the order claims.
