# What "standard monthly income" is

Pension and health insurance are not charged on whatever an employee's gross pay happens to be that month. They run on a "standard monthly income" figure — a fixed base set at the last reset and held steady until the next one, so that a bonus month or a quiet month doesn't move the contribution.

## Where the base comes from

| Insurance | Base set at | Held until |
|---|---|---|
| National pension | July reset (prior year's average income) | Following June |
| Health insurance | April settlement (prior year's confirmed income) | Following March |

A reported pay change can override this early, as covered separately.

## The trap

**Standard monthly income is not the same figure as gross pay on the payslip.** A month with heavy overtime does not raise that month's pension or health contribution, because the base was already fixed at the last reset — the extra pay simply doesn't feed into the deduction until the next annual cycle catches up with it.

That gap between actual pay and the contribution base is exactly why the April and July resets exist in the first place.
