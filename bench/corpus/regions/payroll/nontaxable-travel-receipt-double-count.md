# Travel allowance and a submitted receipt

A trip's daily travel allowance is paid as a fixed, reasonable-cost figure without a receipt. Expensing the same trip's actual costs against a submitted receipt on top of that allowance double counts it.

## Where it goes wrong

The daily allowance (20,000 KRW domestic, USD 50–80 overseas) is treated as reasonable actual cost precisely so nobody has to itemise receipts for ordinary meals and local transport on a trip. **Submitting a receipt for the same day's meals or transport and drawing the daily allowance both pays for the same expense twice** — once as the flat allowance, once as a reimbursed actual cost. Payroll nets the receipt-claimed portion off the allowance for that day rather than paying both in full.

## What is safe to claim separately

Costs the daily allowance was never meant to cover — accommodation, air or rail fares, a client dinner booked and billed separately — go through as ordinary expense claims alongside the untouched daily allowance. The trap is same-expense overlap, not travel expenses in general.
