# Allowance vs reimbursement — tax and evidence at a glance

| Mechanism | Cap | Evidence needed | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standing allowance | 200,000 KRW a month (meal, own-car, childcare, research) | None per payment; qualifying condition confirmed once | Tax free up to the cap, excess taxed as income |
| Reimbursement | No fixed cap | Receipt and business purpose, submitted per claim | Tax free if evidenced; taxed as ordinary income if not |

## The one figure that looks the same but isn't

A round-sum payment labelled "reimbursement" but submitted without receipts is not treated as a reimbursement at all. **Payroll cannot tell a genuine repaid cost from an allowance in disguise, so an unevidenced claim is taxed exactly like ordinary salary — the label on the request doesn't decide the tax treatment, the paperwork behind it does.**

This is why a claim missing its receipt isn't simply returned; it is paid, just paid taxable. What the evidence actually has to show is the next step.
