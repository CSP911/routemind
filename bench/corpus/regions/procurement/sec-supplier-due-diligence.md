# Supplier due diligence — where to start

Before any new supplier is registered or paid, procurement runs a due-diligence pass that sits upstream of the registration form itself. This section is where that pass lives: what gets checked, what standard the supplier is held to, and what stops the relationship before it starts.

## What this covers

Four pages sit under this one:

- **Sanctions and ownership checks** — the screening run against the supplier entity and its beneficial owners
- **Supplier code of conduct** — the standard every registered supplier is required to accept
- **What disqualifies a supplier** — the conditions that end a review outright, win or lose on price
- a fourth page covering the ongoing side of the same checks, re-run rather than one-off

## The common case

Most suppliers clear this in a single pass: the entity checks out, the code of conduct is signed alongside the registration form, and the file moves straight to vendor registration. The checks exist for the exceptions, not the routine cases, but every supplier goes through the same gate regardless of size or spend.

## The one thing that goes wrong

**Due diligence is treated as a one-time gate at onboarding, when ownership and sanctions status can change well after a supplier is registered.** A supplier that cleared cleanly two years ago is not guaranteed to clear today, and nothing in the registration form re-checks that automatically.

Once a supplier clears this section, the file moves to vendor registration proper — where the checks here become one of the attachments the form asks for.
