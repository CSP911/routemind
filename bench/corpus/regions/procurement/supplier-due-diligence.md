# Supplier due diligence

Due diligence happens before a vendor is registered, not after. The registration form (see vendor-form) collects the paperwork; due diligence decides whether that paperwork is even worth collecting.

## The three checks

- A sanctions and ownership check against the current restricted-party lists
- A supplier code of conduct acknowledgement, signed by the supplier
- A basic disqualification screen — has this entity or its owners been flagged before

## What happens on a hit

A sanctions hit or a refused code of conduct stops registration outright. There is no delegated sign-off that overrides it — not the team lead, not the division head, not the CEO. **A hit found after registration is treated the same as a hit found before it**: the vendor is suspended and any open purchase orders are frozen, regardless of how far along the contract is.

## Who runs it

The purchasing desk runs the initial check when the vendor form comes in. Anything ambiguous — a shared director, a holding company two layers up — goes to the division head handling the account, not back to the requester.

The two checks that make up this screen — sanctions and ownership, and the code of conduct — are covered separately below.
