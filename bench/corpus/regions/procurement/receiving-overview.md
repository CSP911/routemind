# Receiving and inspection

A delivery is not accepted just because a courier hands over a box. Whoever receives goods checks them against the purchase order — item, quantity, and condition — before signing the courier's or vendor's delivery note. If the person who placed the order isn't the one receiving the goods, the delivery note and any discrepancies still route back to them, not to whoever happened to be at the desk.

Three things commonly go wrong at this stage: fewer units arrive than ordered, something arrives damaged, or what arrives doesn't match the order closely enough to accept at all. Each has its own handling, covered separately, but all three start the same way — nothing gets signed for as "received in full and good condition" until it's actually been opened and counted.

**Signing the delivery slip is treated as accepting the delivery as described on it.** A signature given because the driver is waiting, before the boxes are opened, forfeits the ability to raise a damage or shortage claim later — the paper trail says everything arrived fine.

What gets recorded at this point feeds directly into the goods receipt note, which is what invoicing checks against before payment goes out.
