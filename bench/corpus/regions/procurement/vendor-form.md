# Vendor registration form

One sheet the supplier fills in, submitted with their papers. The field-by-field detail is on the page
below.

## What the form is for
It is the record the payment system uses. Everything on it is copied into the supplier master, so a
figure that is wrong here is wrong on every invoice afterwards — and correcting a supplier master
after invoices exist is considerably more work than filling the sheet in carefully once.

## Fill it in from the documents, not from memory
The trading name, the representative and the business type all have to match the **business
registration certificate exactly**. The name on the shopfront, the name on the email signature and the
registered name are often three different things, and only one of them is right here.

## Payment terms
Left blank, they default to month-end close and payment at the end of the following month. Anything
else needs a reason written on the form, and it is agreed with the desk rather than with the supplier
— a supplier asking for seven-day terms is a negotiation, not a form field.

## What travels with it
The business registration certificate, a copy of the bank book, the corporate registry extract for a
company, and the national ID consent for a sole trader or freelancer. A form arriving without its
attachments is not partially processed; it waits.

## The rejection to avoid
**The account holder not matching the registered name.** Check it against the bank book copy before
submitting. With a letter of authority it is fine; without one it comes straight back, and the two
working days start again.
