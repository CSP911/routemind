# Vendor registration

How a supplier the company has not used before is set up so they can be ordered from and paid. The
steps are on the page below and the form under that.

## Why it comes first
A supplier that is not registered cannot be paid. Not "is slower to pay" — cannot: there is no
counterparty record for the invoice to attach to. So registration is not paperwork that runs alongside
the purchase, it is the thing that has to finish before an order exists.

## How long it takes
**Two working days** for a domestic supplier, **five** for an overseas one. Plan for it. The most
common delay in the whole area is not the approval, it is discovering on the day of the order that the
supplier was never registered.

## What you have to collect from them
Their business registration certificate, bank details, and for a company the corporate registry
extract. Two cases need something extra:
- **Sole traders and freelancers** are subject to withholding, so a consent form for their national ID
  is needed as well.
- **Overseas suppliers** need the invoicing address and the SWIFT code.

## The thing that gets forms rejected
**The bank account holder must match the registered name.** A shop trading under one name with an
account in the owner's personal name needs a letter of authority. This is the top rejection reason by
a wide margin, and it is worth checking before you submit rather than after.

## One-off purchases
Still need registration. "It is only this once" is the single most common cause of delay here, and the
answer never changes.
