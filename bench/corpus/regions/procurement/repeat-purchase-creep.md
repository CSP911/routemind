# A repeat purchase that crossed a band

A team ordered printing supplies from the same vendor roughly monthly, each order around 350,000 KRW — comfortably inside the team-lead band. Over eight months, order sizes drifted up as the team grew, and the ninth order came in at 520,000 KRW. It was submitted and approved exactly like the previous eight: team lead sign-off, one quote, no comparison, no re-check against the vendor's code-of-conduct signature.

## What was missed

**Nothing about the ninth order looked unusual on its own — it was the first one to cross 500,000 KRW, and the person approving it had no reason to know that.** The division head band requires two quotes and, separately, the vendor should have already signed the supplier code of conduct the moment any order to them reached the 500,000 KRW line, which this one did.

The fix was procedural, not disciplinary: recurring low-value vendors are now flagged for a running-total check at the request stage, so a crossing order is caught before approval rather than noticed afterward in a spend review. The same running-total habit is what catches a purchase quietly split across two orders to dodge a band, and an annual agreement quoted as a monthly figure — all three look identical from inside a single request form.
