# Evidence

What has to be attached for a spend to be accepted. The list of what qualifies, and the ceiling for
each kind, are on the page below.

## Why there are two standards
Below 30,000 KRW a simple receipt is enough. Above it, the tax rules require a document the tax office
can trace — a tax invoice, an invoice, a card slip or a properly issued cash receipt. **Without one,
the spend still happens but the company pays a 2% penalty on it**, which is why a claim without
qualifying evidence is refused rather than absorbed.

## The three ways people lose a claim
1. **A cash receipt taken against a mobile number.** It lands as a personal deduction and can never
   become a company cost. This is the most common rejection by a wide margin, and it cannot be fixed
   afterwards — the receipt has to be reissued by the merchant, and most will not.
2. **A simple receipt for something over 30,000 KRW.** Ask for a tax invoice at the time; almost every
   merchant can issue one.
3. **A transfer record as evidence.** A bank transfer proves money moved, not what it bought. It is not
   evidence of anything the tax office recognises.

## What to do when you genuinely have nothing
A disbursement form, for small amounts — roughly up to 100,000 KRW, at the division head's discretion,
with a written account of what was bought and why there is no document. It is not a way around the
rule; it is the narrow case where the rule cannot be met and the amount is small enough not to matter.

If it was paid by card, you are almost certainly fine. Check the statement before assuming a claim is
lost — the slip is evidence even when the paper receipt is gone.
