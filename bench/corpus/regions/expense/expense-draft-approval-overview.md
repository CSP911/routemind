# Spending that needs a draft first

Most purchases follow the caps already set for the card, a quote, or a claim. Two situations sit outside that: a spend big enough that it needs sign-off before it happens, and a spend that had to happen before anyone could sign off at all.

## Before the spend: pre-approval draft

Once a single purchase crosses the threshold for its category, a short draft goes to the approver first. It states the amount, the vendor, and the reason, and needs a signature before the card is used or the invoice is paid.

## After the spend: regularising an emergency

An emergency spend — the card was blocked overseas, a supplier walked away without a signed PO, a repair couldn't wait for a division head to check email — still needs the same draft, filed once the dust settles instead of before.

**A draft filed after the fact for something that could have waited for approval is not an emergency regularisation — it's a late request, and the approver can decline it outright.** The distinction is whether waiting would have caused real loss, not whether waiting was inconvenient.

Which desk actually reviews either draft is set by the amount, the same way it is for a cash advance.
