# Band assignment across a multi-country trip

## The rule
Each night's lodging and allowance are billed at the band of the country the traveller is actually in when that night is spent, not the band of the trip's primary destination. A five-night trip that opens in Tokyo and closes in Manila claims Tokyo's band for the Tokyo nights and Manila's band for the Manila nights — never one blended rate for the whole itinerary.

## Table
| Band | Example region | Lodging cap | Daily allowance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | North America, Western Europe, Japan | 150,000 KRW | 40,000 KRW |
| 2 | Rest of Asia-Pacific, Eastern Europe | 120,000 KRW | 35,000 KRW |
| 3 | Rest of world | 100,000 KRW | 30,000 KRW |

## The trap
**A travel day that crosses a border mid-flight is billed at the band of the country where the traveller lands and sleeps that night, not the one they took off from.** Filing the whole itinerary at the higher of the two bands, on the assumption that the more generous rate should cover a mixed day, is the single most common overstatement on a multi-country claim.

Where the trip adds a layover long enough to need its own hotel night, that stopover picks up a band of its own.
