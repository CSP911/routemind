# Overseas trip cases: the band-and-country grid

Every overseas claim starts from two coordinates: which country the traveller is in, and which cost band that country sits in for lodging and the daily allowance. The band sets the caps; the country matters only insofar as it maps to one.

## What decides the band

| Situation | Which band applies |
|---|---|
| One country, one class of travel | That country's band, for the whole trip |
| Several countries on one itinerary | Each leg's own band, day by day |
| A country with no explicit listing | The regional default band |

## The common case

Most claims are one traveller, one country, no band change in either direction. The claim system reads the destination and applies the band without anyone opening the mapping table.

## The trap

**A trip that climbs into a higher band partway through does not raise the earlier days retroactively — each day is banded on where the traveller actually was, not on the trip's highest point.**

## Where the cases below start

The pages under this section work through the situations where a single band stops being enough: a second country added mid-route, a stopover long enough to earn its own night, a band change confirmed after departure, a trip that started before a rate move. Match the case to what actually changed, not to how many countries appear on the itinerary.
