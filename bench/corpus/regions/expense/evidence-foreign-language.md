# Foreign-language receipts

## What is accepted
A receipt in English, Japanese or Chinese is accepted as-is if it shows the merchant name, the date and the amount. Receipts in other scripts need a short cover note in English or Korean stating what was bought — a full translation is not required.

## Currency and the 30,000 KRW line
Convert the amount using the transaction date's exchange rate shown on the card statement, not a rate you look up separately. **The 30,000 KRW threshold for qualifying evidence applies to the converted amount, not the face value printed on the receipt.** A ¥3,000 dinner slip can cross the line once converted, even though it looks small on paper.

## When the receipt itself is thin
Overseas vendors often issue a slip with no business registration number at all, since they have no Korean one to print. That's fine for a simple receipt under the converted 30,000 KRW; above it, attach the card statement line alongside the receipt as supporting evidence, since there is no Korean tax invoice to ask for.
