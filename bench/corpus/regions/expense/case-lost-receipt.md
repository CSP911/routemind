# Case — receipts lost on an overseas trip (March 2026, Vietnam)

## What happened
A five-day trip. The hotel receipt (USD 520) and some local transport receipts were lost. Only the card
slips remained.

## How it was settled
| Item | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Hotel, USD 520 | Accepted on **the card slip plus the booking confirmation email**. A card slip is qualifying evidence, so the paper receipt was not needed |
| Local transport, USD 45 (cash) | No evidence → settled on a **disbursement form**. Small, and inside the daily allowance, so the division head closed it out |
| Meals | Covered by the daily allowance, so no evidence was ever required |

## What this taught us
- **Losing the receipt for something paid by card does not matter.** The card slip is enough.
- What causes trouble is **what was paid in cash**. Overseas, it is better to keep cash spending inside
  the daily allowance.
- A disbursement form substitutes only up to about 100,000 KRW. Above that, qualifying evidence is
  needed.

This was not an exception to the evidence rule — it was **meeting it with a card slip**.
