# A conference fee paid abroad, then partly refunded

An employee registered for an overseas conference at 250 USD, charged to the card. The statement showed the transaction-date rate, converting it to roughly 330,000 KRW — well above the 30,000 KRW line, so the invoice from the organiser stood as qualifying evidence on its own.

## What happened next

Part of the programme was cancelled, and the organiser refunded 100 USD two weeks later. The bank credited it at that day's rate, not the original one — the KRW amount didn't match a simple reversal of the first conversion.

## The trap

**You report the KRW figure the bank actually credited on the statement, not the amount you'd get by converting the refunded dollars back at the original charge-date rate.** Recalculating at the old rate produces a number that won't reconcile with either statement, and finance will ask for the correction.

The refund itself still had to be reported within the usual 5 working days, same as any other refund landing after settlement.
