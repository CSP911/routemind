# A flight approved on cost grounds, flagged on emissions

A team lead's Seoul–Busan trip in September was booked as air after a mode-choice approval went through — the traveller had a same-day return connecting to a client meeting, and rail would have cut the visit too short. The fare claim itself was correct and closed without issue.

## What went missing
The trip claim's emissions entry recorded only the domestic air leg. No rail comparison line was filed, because the mode-choice approval had already cleared the booking and nobody thought a second entry was still needed.

## What the audit caught
The claim surfaced in the October sample. The finding wasn't the fare — that was properly approved — it was the missing rail comparison figure required on every domestic leg with a rail option. The team lead filed the correction within the response window, adding the rail-equivalent distance and factor alongside the air entry already on file.

## The lesson
**An approved mode-choice exception settles the fare. It does not settle the emissions entry** — that still needs its own line, filed separately, even when the booking itself was never in question.
