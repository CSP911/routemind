# When the statement name doesn't match the receipt

## Check before you dispute
A card statement often shows the payment processor or the parent company, not the shop or restaurant you actually paid — a hotel booked through a platform, a taxi app billing under its holding company, a small café processing through a POS reseller. This is normal and is not evidence of an unrecognised charge.

## What actually confirms it
Match the amount and the date against your receipt first. If both line up, the charge is legitimate regardless of the name on the statement, and it's claimed as normal with that receipt attached. **Do not raise a dispute purely because the trading name looks unfamiliar** — a dispute raised and later withdrawn still costs the same review time as a genuine one, and the desk that handles them tracks how many come back unfounded.

## If it genuinely doesn't match
Only once the amount, the date, or both fail to line up with anything you spent does this become an unrecognised charge, and the seven-day window for raising it starts from the statement date, not from when you noticed.

The window itself, and what happens once it closes, is covered separately.
