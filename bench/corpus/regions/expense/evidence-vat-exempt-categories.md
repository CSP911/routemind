# VAT-exempt categories and what they issue

## Why some sellers don't charge VAT
A handful of categories are exempt from VAT by law, which means they never issue a tax invoice — not because they're unwilling, but because the transaction sits outside the tax entirely. Common ones on a claim:

| Category | What it issues instead |
|---|---|
| Medical and dental services | A receipt or cash receipt, no VAT line |
| Financial and insurance services | A statement or confirmation, no VAT line |
| Certain tutoring and educational services | A receipt, no VAT line |
| Residential rent | A rent receipt, no VAT line |

## The threshold doesn't move
**None of this raises the 30,000 KRW line.** A cash receipt or plain receipt from any seller on this list, above 30,000 KRW, still has to carry enough detail to stand as qualifying evidence, or the claim carries the same 2% penalty as any other unevidenced spend.

## What to check
Ask the seller for whatever exemption document they normally issue — most have one — before assuming a statement of the circumstances is needed. It usually isn't.

This is why a VAT-exemption receipt from a clinic or a landlord is handled no differently from an ordinary card slip once it clears 30,000 KRW.
