# Rail vs air on marginal domestic legs

Rail is the default for any domestic trip where a rail line exists. Air is only booked when it is genuinely necessary — no rail connection, a same-day return that rail cannot fit into the working day, or a time saving large enough to be worth the fare difference.

## When air is allowed

| Situation | Mode |
|---|---|
| Rail line exists, travel time comparable | Rail |
| No rail connection (e.g. Jeju) | Air |
| Same-day return, rail would not allow same-day travel | Air |
| Marginal time saving, rail available | Rail — air needs prior approval |

**The trap is booking air first and justifying it afterwards.** On a marginal leg — where air saves an hour or two over rail but costs several times more — approval has to be sought before the ticket is bought, not written into the expense report as an afterthought. A fare receipt is not evidence that the mode itself was approved.

Once the mode is settled, the fare is capped the same way as any other trip: qualifying evidence above 30,000 KRW, a simple receipt below it. See the lodging caps by city for the other half of a multi-leg trip.
