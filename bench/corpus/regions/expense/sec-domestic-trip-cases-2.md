# Domestic trip cases, sorted by mode

This section holds fifteen pages of domestic trip cases organised by how the trip travelled: rail against air on legs short enough to be argued either way, a trip that drives one leg and rails another, and the handful of situations where a mode choice made on cost grounds gets flagged for something else — usually emissions.

## What this part covers
The comparison between modes on a single leg, and trips that mix more than one mode across their itinerary.

## The common case
Most legs have an obvious mode — a same-day round trip within driving distance rails or drives, a trip well beyond that flies — and no comparison is needed.

## Where it breaks
**The one thing that catches people out is treating the marginal-leg comparison as a one-time judgement call** — it's a standing rule with a specific cutover, and booking the more expensive mode without checking it first is what turns into a rejected claim later.

The sister section, sorted by city, covers the same trips from the destination side rather than the transport side.
