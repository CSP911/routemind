# Getting money back without a card

Not every spend goes through the corporate card. Four routes cover the rest, and they solve different problems — confusing them is what sends a claim back.

## The four routes

| Route | Used for | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Disbursement form | Small spend with no receipt and no card | ~100,000 KRW, at the division head's discretion |
| Cash advance / float | Anticipated spend ahead of a trip or project | Set case by case, routed by the approval bands |
| Petty cash | Recurring small office spend | A per-withdrawal and monthly cap set by the custodian |
| Personal-card reimbursement | A business charge that landed on someone's own card | Same evidence rule as any other spend — no lighter threshold |

## The trap

**A disbursement form and a reimbursement claim are not the same thing.** The form substitutes for missing evidence on a small spend; a reimbursement pays back money that was already spent and already has a receipt. Filing one where the other belongs is the single most common reason these claims bounce.

The form's own fields are covered separately — what follows here is the advance, the float, and paying a personal card back.
