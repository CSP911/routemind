# Allowances that ride on pay

Some support for everyday costs never touches the corporate card or a claim form at all — it lands in the payslip alongside salary. The meal allowance, the own-car allowance and the remote-work stipend all work this way: HR sets the monthly figure, payroll adds it automatically, and nothing needs a receipt.

## How this differs from a card charge or a reimbursement

A card charge or a reimbursement pays back an actual, evidenced cost. A payroll allowance pays a fixed amount regardless of what you actually spent that month. You don't submit a form to draw it, and you don't submit receipts to justify it — eligibility is what triggers it, not spend.

## Where the line sits

**Each of these allowances has a non-taxable ceiling set by tax rule, not by company policy — cross it and the excess is taxed exactly like ordinary salary, not like a reimbursement.** Two of the three allowances here sit right at their ceiling; the third has no ceiling at all and is taxable from the first won. Which is which matters more than the headline amount.

The individual rate tables and the eligibility rules for each allowance sit in the pages that follow — start with the meal allowance, since it's the one most people already draw without knowing it has a limit.
