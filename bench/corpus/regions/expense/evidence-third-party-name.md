# A receipt in someone else's name

## The problem
A card slip, invoice or tax invoice addressed to someone other than the claimant — a colleague's name on a hotel booking, a shared taxi paid through someone else's app — does not qualify as your evidence, even if you reimburse them separately afterward.

## What to do instead
Have the actual payer file the claim, then settle between yourselves outside the expense system. If the company must pay you directly because you fronted the cash, attach a note explaining the arrangement together with the third party's receipt — accounting may still ask for the payer's confirmation.

## The trap
**A receipt in a colleague's name attached to your own claim is rejected outright, not merely flagged** — it is treated the same as no evidence at all, which above 30,000 KRW means the 2% penalty if it slips through unqueried.

This is a different failure from splitting one bill across several smaller payments to dodge the threshold, which is its own trap.
