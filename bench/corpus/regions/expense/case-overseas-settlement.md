# Case — an overseas settlement, end to end

A worked example of a trip that went wrong in the ordinary way, kept because the reasoning is more
useful than the rule. The detail of the March 2026 Vietnam trip is on the page below; this is what it
is an example *of*.

## Why this case is kept
Because almost every difficult overseas claim is the same shape: **some things paid by card, some in
cash, and the paper receipts gone.** People assume the lost paper is the problem. It is not. The card
slips settle themselves. What has to be decided is the cash.

## The reasoning to copy
1. **Sort the spending by how it was paid, not by what it was for.** Card first — those are already
   evidenced and need nothing further.
2. **For the cash, ask whether the daily allowance already covers it.** If it does, there is nothing to
   claim and nothing to prove. Meals almost always fall here.
3. **What is left is the actual question.** Small, and a disbursement form with a written account will
   close it. Large, and qualifying evidence is needed however inconvenient that is.

## The boundary
A disbursement form substitutes for evidence up to roughly 100,000 KRW, at the division head's
discretion. It is not a general escape — used twice for the same kind of spend it stops being an
exception and starts being a pattern, and the desk will say so.

## What to do differently next time
Pay by card. Where cash is unavoidable, keep it inside the allowance. Both of those are decisions made
on the trip, not at settlement, which is the only reason this page is worth reading before you go.
