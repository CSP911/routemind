# Who signs off a disbursement form

## The role
Approval sits with the division head, exercised case by case rather than against a fixed list of merchants or spend types. It is a judgement call on whether the circumstances genuinely leave no better evidence available — not a rubber stamp for anything under the ceiling.

## Escalation
If the claimant's own division head is the payee — a condolence gift the head himself is receiving, for instance — approval moves up to the next level rather than being self-signed.

## The trap
**Discretion applies to whether to accept the form, not to how high the ceiling goes.** A division head cannot approve a disbursement form in place of qualifying evidence above roughly 100,000 KRW just because the circumstances seem reasonable; the ceiling is fixed, and the discretion sits entirely below it.

Where the spend is larger and the problem is a missing tax invoice rather than a small undocumented one, the approval route is a statement of circumstances, not this form.
