# A trip booked before the restricted-country sign-off landed

A team lead booked flights and lodging for a client visit to a Level 3 country, expecting the exception sign-off to follow as a formality once travel arrangements were already underway. It didn't arrive before departure.

## What happened
The division head reviewing the request wanted a revised itinerary that avoided an overland leg through a neighbouring region flagged separately. That took four extra days to arrange. The flights, already booked, covered the original dates.

## Why it mattered
**Travel insurance does not attach to a Level 3 destination until the exception is recorded as signed off — booking the trip first does not bring the cover forward to match.** For those four days the itinerary sat approved-in-principle but uncovered. Had anything happened during that window, the policy would not have responded.

## Resolution
The original flights were cancelled and rebooked against the revised itinerary once sign-off landed, at a modest change fee absorbed by the trip budget rather than claimed as a loss. The lesson written up afterward: request the exception before booking anything, not alongside it.

A Level 4 destination does not admit even this workaround — no revised itinerary changes that outcome.
