# Who signs off air on a marginal domestic leg

On a route like Seoul–Daegu, where rail and air fares sit close enough to be a real choice, booking air instead of rail needs the team lead's approval before the ticket is bought. This is a booking-stage sign-off, not a claim-stage one — the point is to catch the decision before money moves, not to review it afterwards.

If the traveller also wants business class rather than economy, that step needs the division head, regardless of whether the mode itself was already cleared.

## What happens without it

**There is no retroactive approval for a marginal leg booked as air without sign-off.** The claim is settled as though the ticket had been the rail fare for that route, and the traveller carries the difference — the approval step exists precisely to stop that gap from appearing after the fact.

Routes with no rail alternative at all, like Jeju, don't go through this step at all, since there's no marginal decision to approve. The mode-choice worked example shows how the Seoul–Daegu comparison is actually run before that approval is sought.
