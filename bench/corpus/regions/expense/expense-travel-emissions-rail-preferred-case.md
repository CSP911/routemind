# A rail exception that still counted against the annual figure

## The trip
A team lead flew Seoul–Busan on an approved mode-choice exception — a same-day return around a client meeting made rail impractical. The claim went through with the fare booked as air, sign-off attached, no issue raised at approval.

## What the emissions line showed
The per-trip entry logged the actual distance at the air factor, then set the rail figure as the comparison the annual report tracks — exactly as the rail preference rule requires, regardless of whether the mode-choice exception had been signed off.

**An approved exception changes what the trip is allowed to book, not what the annual figure compares it against.** The team lead assumed a signed exception took the trip out of scope for emissions tracking entirely. It doesn't — the exception speaks to the fare and the allowance, not the comparison line underneath them.

## Where it landed
The gap between the two factors was added to the division's quarterly total as a marked exception rather than folded in silently, which is why one quarter's figure can show more exceptions than another without the underlying travel volume having changed at all.

This is the same line the mode-choice approver draws when signing off the fare: the exception moves the cost, not the baseline it gets measured against.
