# A prepaid overseas hotel cancelled mid-trip

## The case

A five-night hotel was prepaid on the corporate card in KRW before departure. On night three, the trip was cut short and the remaining two nights were cancelled. The hotel refunded those two nights to the same card, in KRW, four days later.

## How it settled

The three nights actually stayed were checked against the band's lodging cap as usual, using the original KRW invoice as evidence. The refund for the two cancelled nights was reported separately — **within 5 working days of it landing on the card** — rather than folded quietly into a smaller final claim. Reporting it as a claim adjustment instead of a refund would have understated what the card was actually charged and refunded.

## The trap

The traveller's first instinct was to just claim three nights and say nothing about the other two, since the net cost matched. **The refund still has to be reported on its own even when the final lodging total looks correct**, because the settlement checks the invoice and the refund against each other, not the final number alone.

This is the same reporting clock that governs any refund landing after a claim window, whatever the trip's lodging pattern.
