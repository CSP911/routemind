# What else the disbursement form covers

The disbursement form is usually first met as the fix for a lost receipt on an overseas trip. That's one use, not the whole scope.

## Where else it applies

- A vendor that genuinely has no card terminal and no receipt book — a market stall, a one-off cash service.
- A small reimbursement where getting a tax invoice after the fact isn't realistic and the amount sits under the ceiling.
- Any spend under roughly 100,000 KRW where qualifying evidence cannot reasonably be produced, at the division head's discretion.

## The ceiling still holds

| Amount | Route |
|---|---|
| Up to ~100,000 KRW | Disbursement form, division head's discretion |
| Above ~100,000 KRW | Qualifying evidence is required — the form does not substitute |

## The trap

**Treating the disbursement form as a general-purpose workaround for missing evidence is what gets it rejected.** It exists for cases where evidence genuinely can't exist or can't be obtained — not for a spend where a tax invoice was simply not requested at the time. An approver checking the form for a repeat pattern of "no receipt" on the same category is the usual first sign this line is being leaned on too hard.

For the fields the form itself requires, see the disbursement form reference directly.
