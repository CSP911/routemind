# Entertaining with a public official present

A meal, gift or payment involving a public official — a government employee, a public school teacher, a public corporation staff member — is not governed by the company's usual entertainment caps once that person is present. Anti-corruption law sets its own, lower ceilings, and those apply regardless of what the company's own policy allows.

## Who confirms it

The compliance desk has to be notified before the event, not after, whenever a public official is expected to attend. Compliance confirms which of the lower limits applies to the occasion and signs off before booking.

## The trap

**Staying under the company's own 50,000 KRW meal cap or 100,000 KRW gift cap is not enough — the legal ceiling for a public official is lower than the company's own caps, and it does not move just because the spend was within budget.** A dinner that clears the entertainment policy cleanly can still be a violation of anti-corruption law if a public official was at the table and the lower limit was missed.

Where a public official's attendance is only confirmed on the day, stop and check with compliance before the bill is paid rather than after the claim is filed — this is the one case in entertainment spend where the approval has to run ahead of the receipt, not behind it.
