# Travel emissions reporting — where to start

Every booked trip writes an emissions entry alongside its cost entry, whether or not anyone thinks of the trip as an environmental question. This part covers how that entry gets recorded, how it rolls up into the figure the company reports annually, and where policy asks travellers to choose rail over air. The pages below hold the factors and the correction procedure; this page is only the shape.

## What this section covers

- What auto-fills into the emissions entry when a trip is booked
- How individual trip entries roll into the annual figure, and when that figure locks
- Where the policy expects rail to be chosen over air, and what "reasonable" means there

## The common case

Most domestic trips record themselves without anyone touching the emissions field — mode and distance are enough to auto-fill the rest.

## Where it goes wrong

**A flight approved on cost or time grounds still counts against the annual figure.** Approval settles whether the trip could be booked; it does nothing to the emissions entry underneath it. Travellers and approvers both tend to assume that a signed-off exception erases the record, and it doesn't — the entry stands, flagged, and gets totted up with everything else at year-end lockdown.

This entry rides on the same trip record that produces the cost claim, so a mistake here is usually easiest to catch from the settlement side — covered next, in trip days versus allowance days.
