# What a supplier's quote needs to show

## Why a quote gets asked for
Any single purchase over 500,000 KRW needs a supplier's written quote attached before payment goes ahead, whether the spend ends up on the card or moves through a purchase order. The card's own per-transaction cap doesn't excuse it — a team lead's card comfortably covers a 700,000 KRW order, but the order still needs the quote first.

## What the document must include
| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Supplier's registered name | Matches the eventual invoice or receipt |
| Itemised cost | Confirms the price wasn't rounded up after the fact |
| Quote number or reference | Ties the quote to the order it was raised for |
| Date issued and validity period | Sets how long the price is guaranteed |

## The trap
**A price quoted in an email body or a chat message is not a quote**, even if it lists every item and a number. Procurement needs a document the supplier actually issued as one — letterhead, a quote number, a signed PDF — not a paraphrase of a conversation. A screenshot of a chat gets sent back for reissue, which costs the days the purchase didn't need to lose.

## Attaching it
The quote goes into the same request that asks for a PO number, or sits alongside the receipt if the purchase clears on the card instead. Either way it has to predate the purchase, not get requested afterwards to paper over a spend that already happened.

How long that same quote stays valid before the purchase against it needs refreshing is a separate cutoff, not this one.
