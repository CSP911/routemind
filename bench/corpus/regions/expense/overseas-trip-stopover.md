# Whether a stopover counts as a destination

Not every city a traveller passes through is a claimable destination.

## The rule

A connection of under 8 hours with no lodging booked is not a destination in its own right — it takes no band and generates no allowance day of its own; it is absorbed into the leg either side of it.

A layover that requires an overnight — including one the airline schedules rather than one you chose — is a real lodging night and must be claimed against that stopover country's own band.

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Under 8 hours, no lodging | No separate band, no allowance day |
| Overnight required, airline-scheduled | Full lodging night, banded by that country |
| Overnight, traveller's own choice to break the journey | Same as above — banded by that country |

## The trap

**An airline-forced overnight is still your overnight for claim purposes.** Treating it as "not really part of the trip" and skipping the lodging line is the wrong direction of error — it usually means an unclaimed night, not an overclaimed one, but it also means the allowance for that day goes unclaimed if nobody notices.
