# What the draft has to show

A draft is short by design — it exists to get a decision, not to document the whole purchase. Procurement or the approver will still ask for the supplier's quote or the receipt separately once the spend goes through.

## What every draft needs

- Who is requesting it, and their team
- The vendor or payee
- The amount and currency
- A one-line business reason
- The budget line it will be charged against
- The date approval is requested — before the spend, except for an emergency draft

**Leaving off the budget line is the most common reason a draft bounces back unsigned — the approver has no way to check it against what's left of the ceiling for that line, and won't guess.**

## The one extra field for an emergency

An emergency draft, filed after the spend, adds a single line: what made it impossible to get sign-off first. "The supplier wouldn't hold the booking" is enough; "it was more convenient" is not, and an approver who reads it that way will reject the draft rather than back-date it.

Where the money for that spend actually sits once it's approved is set the same way as any other line — the same categories a team's travel and entertainment spend already map onto.
