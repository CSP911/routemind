# What the variance narrative has to say

## The three parts
Finance rejects a narrative that doesn't cover all three of these, regardless of which band the variance falls into:

| Field | What it has to state |
|---|---|
| Cause | The specific reason for the gap — a named vendor, a timing shift, a cancelled trip |
| Corrective action | What changes, if anything, before the next report |
| Expected trend | Whether the gap is one-off or likely to repeat next month |

## The trap
**"Spend was higher than expected" is not a cause — it restates the variance instead of explaining it, and finance sends it back.** A narrative has to name what actually drove the number, even briefly; a vendor invoice landing early, a trip that moved from next quarter, a subscription renewal that wasn't reforecast.

## Why it's checked here and not later
The narrative is what a division head reads before deciding whether a sign-off is even needed on a borderline line — a vague cause makes that call harder, not easier, and tends to bounce the report back before it reaches sign-off at all.

The bands that decide which variances need this narrative in the first place sit in the threshold table.
