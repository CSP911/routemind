# What goes on a disbursement form

## Fields
| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Date | Date of the actual spend, not the filing date |
| Payee | Who received the money |
| Amount | The full amount paid |
| Reason | Business purpose, in enough detail to stand alone |
| Approval | Division head's signature |

## Where it fits
The form exists to cover small, awkward spends where no tax invoice, receipt, or card slip was ever going to exist — a cash gift, a one-off vendor with no register. It is signed off at the division head's discretion, case by case, not against a fixed list.

## The trap
**The form only substitutes for evidence up to roughly 100,000 KRW.** Above that, qualifying evidence is still required regardless of how well the form is filled in or who signs it — discretion covers whether to accept the form below the line, not whether to move the line. A disbursement form for 150,000 KRW with no invoice behind it is rejected the same as a bare receipt.

If the shortfall is a missing tax invoice rather than a small cash spend, a statement of circumstances is the right document instead.
