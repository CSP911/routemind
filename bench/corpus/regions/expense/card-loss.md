# Lost card and misuse

What to do when the card is gone, and what to do when a charge on the statement is not yours. They are
different problems with different clocks — the ordered steps are on the page below.

## A lost card
The first action is **stopping the card with the issuer**, not telling anyone here. Every minute
between the loss and the stop is the company's exposure, and the issuer's liability process is
measured from the stop. Telling the office & purchasing desk is the second step, not the first.

Reissue takes three to five working days. Unavoidable spending in the meantime goes on a personal card
and is claimed back on a disbursement form — keep the evidence as you normally would, since a personal
card slip is qualifying evidence too.

## A charge that is not yours
**Seven days from the statement.** Raise it inside that and it goes through the issuer's fraud process.
Raise it after, and it is treated as your own spending, because by then it cannot be distinguished
from a charge you simply forgot.

## The distinction worth understanding
A charge you do not recognise is **unexplained**, not fraudulent, until the issuer says otherwise. The
two are handled differently: fraud is the issuer's process and the company does not absorb it up
front; unexplained is yours, and it is recovered from pay at month end. Most charges people report as
fraud turn out to be a subscription they forgot or a merchant trading under another name — which is
why the seven days exists, to look before the statement closes.

If the card was lost with a bag or a wallet, tell the desk what else was in it. Access cards and
laptops have their own procedures and different desks.
