# Regularising an emergency spend

An emergency spend is one that happened before anyone could approve it — not one where approval was simply skipped because it was faster. The point of this procedure is to convert the first into a legitimate claim; it does nothing for the second.

## Steps

1. File the draft as soon as the situation allows, marked as an emergency draft rather than a standard pre-approval.
2. Attach whatever evidence already exists — a receipt, an invoice, even a screenshot of the booking.
3. State plainly what made prior sign-off impossible. Vague reasons get sent back for detail, not approved on trust.
4. Route it to whichever approver would have signed a draft of that size in advance — the amount decides the desk, not the fact that it's after the event.
5. If the approver signs, the spend is treated exactly as if the draft had gone through beforehand. If they decline, it reverts to unauthorised spending, and the person who made it — not the company — carries the cost.

**A draft filed weeks after the spend, once someone remembers it was never approved, is not what this procedure covers — there is a hard window for calling something an emergency, and past it the draft simply won't be accepted as one.**
