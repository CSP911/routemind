# E-receipts and their reissue

## Accepting an e-receipt
A PDF or emailed receipt qualifies exactly like a paper one, provided it shows the merchant, the date and the amount. A screenshot of a payment app confirmation is not enough on its own — the app must also show the merchant's business registration number for anything over 30,000 KRW.

## When it never arrives
Some online merchants send the e-receipt hours or days after payment. File the expense claim on time regardless; attach the e-receipt once it lands, or note on the claim that it is pending.

## Requesting a reissue
1. Contact the merchant's support line or portal directly first — most resend within a day.
2. **If the merchant can't resend it, file a statement of the circumstances as a draft. Reissue through this route takes 3–5 working days**, so start it the moment you notice the receipt is missing, not at month-end close.
3. Attach the reissued document once it arrives and resubmit.

Don't wait on the reissue before touching the claim itself — a merchant that genuinely can't produce a tax invoice at all is a separate problem, covered next.
