# Getting a cancelled leg's refund back to the company

1. Cancel the booking through the same channel it was made in — the booking platform or the carrier's desk, not by simply not travelling.
2. Confirm the refund amount and timing before filing anything; partial refunds (cancellation fees withheld) are common on discounted fares.
3. **Report the refund within 5 working days of it landing on the card or account.** Left unreported, it is treated as undisclosed income against the traveller, the same as any other refund that arrives after a claim has run.
4. If the refund lands after the trip's expense claim has already been settled, file it as a post-settlement refund rather than reopening the original claim.
5. If the leg is rebooked rather than simply cancelled, the new fare is claimed on its own, following the usual rail-versus-air check for that leg.

**Do not net the refund against a different leg's fare on the same claim to save a step.** Each leg is evidenced and reported on its own; netting two legs together is exactly the kind of adjustment that gets a claim sent back for detail.

Where the cancellation happens because the whole trip is called off, the deposits and prepayments already on the card follow the same refund-reporting rule.
