# Rail vs air on a marginal leg — a worked case

## The trip
A Seoul–Daegu day trip for a vendor audit. KTX economy runs 43,500 KRW each way; the earliest same-day flight comes to 95,000 KRW once the airport transfer is added in. Neither departure gets the traveller there materially earlier once check-in time is counted.

## Why rail wins by default
The mode-choice rule treats rail as the default whenever a same-day KTX connection exists. Air only clears without an exception when no rail link covers the route in time, or when the fare difference is trivial next to the schedule gain. Here it's neither: more than double the fare for no real time saved.

## The trap
**Booking the flight first and asking for the exception afterward doesn't work — an air fare booked without prior approval is reimbursed only up to what the rail fare would have cost**, and the gap comes out of the traveller's own pocket. The exception has to be signed off before the ticket is bought, not argued after the fact.

## What to check
Compare against the standard fare table before booking anything. Which desk actually signs that exception off is a separate question, covered on its own page.
