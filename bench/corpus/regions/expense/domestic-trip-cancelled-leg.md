# A cancelled leg

A rail or air leg gets cancelled — by the traveller, because plans changed, or by the carrier. Either way the ticket cost has to be resolved, not just left sitting on the card statement.

## Steps

1. Cancel through the same channel the ticket was booked on, and keep the cancellation confirmation — it stands in place of the original receipt.
2. If a refund is issued, **report it within 5 working days of the refund landing on the card or account.** Left unreported, it is treated as undisclosed income against the trip, the same as any other refund that lands after a claim has already run.
3. If a cancellation fee was withheld, that fee is the actual cost of the leg and is claimed instead of the full fare — attach the confirmation showing the fee.
4. If the leg is rebooked rather than simply cancelled, claim the new fare and keep both confirmations; do not claim the cancelled fare and the rebooked one as two separate legs.

**A cancellation that produces no refund and no fee — the ticket simply expires unused — still needs the confirmation attached.** A trip report with a fare charge and no travel to show for it, and no cancellation record either, reads as an unused ticket nobody accounted for.
