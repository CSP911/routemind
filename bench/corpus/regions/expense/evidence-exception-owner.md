# Who processes each awkward-case exception

## Three different desks
Not every exception is decided by the same person. The claimant files the paperwork, but who reviews it depends on what kind of exception it is.

| Exception | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Statement of the circumstances (lost, unrecoverable, no tax invoice) | Division head, on the merits of the statement |
| Disbursement form (small undocumented spend) | Division head, within the roughly 100,000 KRW ceiling |
| Pending e-receipt reissue | Accounting, tracking the 3–5 working day window |
| Refund after settlement | Accounting, on the report filed within 5 working days |

## The trap
**A statement of the circumstances and a disbursement form both land on the division head's desk, but they are not interchangeable — a disbursement form only works below the roughly 100,000 KRW line, and using one above it doesn't get waved through just because the same person signs both.**

## Why this matters
Filing the wrong document to the wrong process usually means a second round of paperwork rather than a rejection, but it costs the days that the underlying deadline doesn't stop counting through.

This is worth knowing before the statement queue backs up near a filing deadline.
