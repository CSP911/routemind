# Evidence for a personal-card reimbursement claim

A charge that happened to land on someone's own card is still a business spend, and it is evidenced exactly like one.

## The line that doesn't move

| Amount | What's needed |
|---|---|
| Up to 30,000 KRW | A simple receipt is fine |
| Over 30,000 KRW | Qualifying evidence — a tax invoice, an invoice, or a card slip |
| No qualifying evidence available | A statement of circumstances, filed as a draft |

If qualifying evidence genuinely cannot be produced, the statement-of-circumstances route applies here too, with the same reissue window as any other spend.

## The trap

**Paying with a personal card does not create a lighter evidence standard just because the receipt exists.** People treat a personal-card charge as self-evidently real because the employee is out of pocket, and skip asking for the tax invoice at the point of sale. By the time the claim is filed, the merchant may no longer be reachable to reissue it, and the 2% penalty lands on the reimbursement instead.

Ask for the qualifying document the moment the charge happens — reissuing it later is always slower and sometimes impossible.
