# Evidence from a private individual

## Not every seller is a business
Some counterparties — a freelance instructor, someone selling secondhand equipment, a private landlord — have no business registration number at all and cannot issue a tax invoice, an invoice, or a card slip, because there is no business behind the sale.

## Below and above the line
Below 30,000 KRW nothing changes — a simple receipt, or a note of the transfer, is accepted as normal. Above it, none of the four qualifying documents can exist. **A bank transfer record on its own does not qualify — it shows money moved, not what it was for — so this still needs a statement of the circumstances, not a screenshot of the transfer.**

## What to attach instead
File the statement as a draft, describing the seller, the amount and the reason no tax invoice can exist. It is reviewed the same way as any other statement, and the same 2% penalty applies if the claim is settled without it.

The reasoning is the same as for a registered business that simply lacks the setup to issue qualifying evidence — the difference here is that there was never a business to register.
