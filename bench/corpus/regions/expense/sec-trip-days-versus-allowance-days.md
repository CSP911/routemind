# Trip days versus allowance days — where to start

A trip has a start date and an end date. Separately, it has a set of days that actually qualify for a daily allowance. Most of the time these two counts are identical, which is exactly why the cases where they aren't catch people off guard. This part covers the four situations where the split shows up — a trip confirmed late, a day attended but never claimed, a weekend day taken in lieu, and a trip that crosses a month boundary. The twelve pages below work each one through; this page only names the shape.

## What this section covers

- How a late confirmation affects which days count
- The gap between being present on a day and having it appear on the claim
- Weekend travel days taken back as time off instead of paid
- What happens to a trip's day count when it straddles month close

## The common case

A three- or four-day domestic trip where every calendar day is also an allowance day — no split, nothing to reconcile.

## Where it goes wrong

**Attendance is not the same system as the claim, and neither one automatically populates the other.** A day someone was demonstrably on-site can still be missing from the allowance claim if it was never entered, and the reverse — a claimed day with no attendance record — draws exactly the kind of question month-end audit exists to ask.

The month boundary case in particular overlaps with how the close calendar treats an open trip, which is where team ceilings pick the story back up.
