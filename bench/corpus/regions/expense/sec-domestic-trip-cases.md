# Domestic trip cases — where to start

Domestic trip cases group the situations that come up on trips inside the country: which mode to book on a marginal leg, how a trip with two overnight stops is treated, what a client-site day pays when no hotel is booked. The two sections underneath split this ground two ways — by city, and by mode of travel — so a reader can find a case either by where the trip went or by how it got there.

## What this part covers
Everything downstream of the trip request that depends on domestic specifics: city-level lodging and allowance differences, and the rail-versus-air question that recurs on legs short enough to argue about.

## The common case
Most domestic trips settle against a single city and a single mode, and the ordinary caps apply without a case needing to be raised at all.

## Where it breaks
**The one thing worth flagging here is that a trip can belong in both sub-sections at once** — a two-city trip that also drives one leg and rails the other needs both the city table and the mode table read together, not one in place of the other.

The two sections below sit either side of that split; overseas trip cases pick up the same ground once a trip crosses a border.
