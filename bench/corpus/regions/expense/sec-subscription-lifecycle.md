# Subscription lifecycle, start to finish

A subscription has four moments that matter: it gets registered, it renews, someone eventually cancels it, and somewhere in between the seat count stops matching the team that's actually using it. The pages under this one walk each moment in detail — this page is just the map.

## The shape of it

| Stage | Owner | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Requester + budget owner | Subscription registration form |
| Renewal | Budget owner | Who signs off a subscription's renewal |
| Cancellation | Budget owner | Cancelling a subscription before it renews |
| Seat audit | Budget owner + IT | Seat count audit procedure |

## The common case

A team registers a tool, it goes on a card or a purchase order against a cost centre, and it renews quietly on its own cycle for years. Nobody revisits it unless a licence review, a budget variance, or an unrelated reorganisation forces the question.

## Where it breaks

**The trap is ownership drift, not the renewal itself.** The person who registered the subscription is rarely still the one managing it a year on — teams split, people move, people leave — but the subscription keeps renewing against whoever's card or cost centre it was first set up on. By the time anyone notices, months of charges have gone to a team that no longer owns the service, and seat counts have grown or shrunk without anyone adjusting the licence tier to match.

Where the charge itself lands each cycle, and which card or PO it should sit on, is covered under card use by situation.
