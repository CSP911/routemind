# A worked case: a taxi chartered through a typhoon shutdown

A field engineer was stranded when a typhoon cancelled the last train back after a site visit. With no rail option and the office already closed, they chartered a car for 350,000 KRW to get home the same night, rather than book a hotel and wait it out.

## What happened next

The charge went through on a personal card, since the corporate card had already hit its evening limit. Two days later, once the engineer was back and the trains were running, they filed an emergency draft: amount, vendor, and the reason no prior sign-off was possible — the office was unreachable and the alternative was an unplanned overnight stay that would have cost more.

Because 350,000 KRW sits above the 300,000 KRW cash-spend threshold, the draft went to a team lead rather than being self-approved. It was signed the same day, and the engineer claimed the amount through the personal-card reimbursement route once the draft was attached.

**The reimbursement itself still needed the receipt for the charter — the approved draft covers the decision to spend, not the evidence for the spend.** Without the receipt, the claim would have gone through the disbursement form instead, capped well below what the trip actually cost.

Filed a day later, the same draft would have missed the five-working-day window and converted to unauthorised spending regardless of how reasonable the typhoon made it look.
