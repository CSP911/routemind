# A trip that drives one leg and rails the other

A traveller drives a personal car from the office to a regional hub, then takes rail onward to the actual client city, returning the same way.

## What each leg claims

The driven leg claims the standard 300 KRW per km rate plus any tolls, with start point, end point and distance recorded as usual. The rail leg claims the actual fare on the receipt, exactly as it would if the whole trip had been by rail.

Neither leg reduces the other. The mileage rate isn't discounted because part of the journey was covered by public transport, and the rail fare isn't reduced because a car was involved earlier in the day.

**The one line that doesn't work is claiming mileage for the rail portion of the trip, or vice versa** — each leg is paid at the rate for the mode actually used on that leg, and the claim form needs both legs entered separately rather than one blended distance-and-fare figure.

The own-car claim form covers what has to be filled in for the driven portion; the rail-air fare table applies unchanged to whichever leg goes by train.
