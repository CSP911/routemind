# What to do after an incident abroad

1. **Call the insurer's 24-hour assistance line first**, using the number on the policy summary carried in your travel confirmation email. This is not optional paperwork — the assistance line coordinates directly with local hospitals and can arrange direct billing, which a call to your manager cannot do.
2. Notify your approver and the travel desk within 24 hours of the incident.
3. If anything was stolen, file a police report before leaving the jurisdiction — most claims require it and it cannot be obtained retroactively from Korea.
4. Keep every receipt generated by the incident itself: treatment, replacement items, transport.
5. Submit the formal insurance claim within the filing deadline.

## The trap
**Calling your manager first, before the assistance line, is the single most common delay.** It feels like the responsible first move, but it does nothing for emergency coordination and simply pushes the assistance call back by hours — hours that matter for direct billing and evacuation decisions.

How long you actually have to file the formal claim is a separate, harder deadline, covered next.
