# Cash receipts as qualifying evidence

## What it is
A cash receipt (현금영수증) is one of the four documents that satisfy the qualifying-evidence requirement above 30,000 KRW, alongside a tax invoice, an invoice, and a card slip. It's issued by the seller's terminal at the point of sale, tied to an ID number entered at the till.

## Where it goes wrong
Most terminals default to whatever number the customer gives — often a personal mobile number rather than the company's registration number. A cash receipt tied to a personal number is still a valid tax document, but it does not automatically attach to a company claim; it has to be looked up and reissued against the business number, or submitted with enough detail for the claim to be traced back to it.

## The trap
**Asking for "a receipt" at the counter often produces a plain register slip, not a cash receipt.** The two look similar but only one is qualifying evidence above 30,000 KRW — the difference is whether the amount was reported to the tax authority at the time, which a register slip alone does not do.

Sellers who genuinely can't issue any tax document at all are a separate case, covered on the no-tax-invoice page.
