# Overseas trip cases at a glance

## Why one case page isn't enough

A single overseas trip can trigger several of the recurring cases at once — a multi-country itinerary that also crosses a rate move, or a stopover that turns into a band change. Each situation has its own confirming desk and its own deadline, set out on its own page. This table is the fast way to see which mechanic applies before you start filing.

## The six mechanics

| Situation | What changes | Who confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-country trip | Band and currency reset at each border, night by night | Traveller tags each night's country |
| Band change mid-trip | The new band applies from the change date forward, not retroactively | Approver, before the new leg starts |
| Stopover | A long layover can earn its own band and lodging night | Layover duration, not traveller preference |
| Trip spanning a rate move | Each charge converts at its own transaction date, not one blended rate | Traveller, by tagging charges either side of the move |
| Prepaid lodging billed at home | The KRW charge is reconciled against the band cap once actual nights are known | Settlement, once the trip is over |
| Per-diem day with no attendance | Whether the day pays out depends on whether it counts as attended | Approver, on the borderline days |

**The trap is treating all six as one "note it on the form" problem.** Filing under a single blended band or a single exchange rate for the whole trip is the most common reason a claim comes back — each mechanic has its own confirming step, and none of them defaults in your favour.

For the deadlines behind each one, see the individual case page; this table only tells you which mechanic you're dealing with.
