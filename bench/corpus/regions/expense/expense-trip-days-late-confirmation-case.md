# Late confirmation and the day count

## The scenario
A three-day domestic trip departed on a Monday before approval came through — the request was still sitting with the team lead when the traveller left. Approval was granted on Tuesday, backdated to Monday's departure. The trip claim system, though, defaults to counting allowance days from the approval date forward, not from the actual departure.

## What actually happened
Monday's allowance did not populate automatically. The traveller had to add it manually to the claim, referencing the backdated approval and the boarding pass as evidence that travel began that day.

## The trap
**A backdated approval does not automatically backdate the allowance days in the system.** The approval date fixes accountability; the traveller still has to correct the day count by hand or lose the first day's allowance entirely.

## What to check before filing
- The approval note states the actual departure date, not the date it was signed.
- Every allowance day claimed has a corresponding travel or attendance record.

This is the same manual step needed when a same-day trip unexpectedly turns into an overnight stay — the system doesn't extend the claim on its own either.
