# A ride receipt issued to someone else's name

## What happened
A team lead's assistant booked and paid for a client ride using a taxi app registered under the assistant's own account, since the app doesn't support switching payment profiles mid-trip. The receipt that came through was addressed to the assistant, not the team lead who was actually travelling.

## Why it was rejected
**The receipt named the assistant, and the trip was claimed under the team lead — that mismatch was rejected outright, exactly as any third-party-name receipt would be, regardless of who actually paid.**

## How it was fixed
The assistant re-filed the claim under their own name instead, since they were the one the receipt actually evidenced, and the team lead's trip cost was reimbursed to the assistant separately by internal transfer rather than through the expense system.

## The lesson
When a booking has to run through someone else's account, the claim needs to follow the name on the receipt, not the name of whoever benefited from the ride.

This is the same rule that applies to a shared taxi where only one rider's card is charged.
