# Who confirms a client-site trip carried no lodging claim

When a trip involves an overnight stay at a client site but the client provides the room, no lodging line is filed at all — only the fare and the daily allowance apply, the same as any other client-site trip. The approver reviewing the claim is responsible for checking that this is actually the case before signing off, since the claim system doesn't know on its own whether a room was provided.

## Where this goes wrong

**A lodging claim filed "in case it's needed," against a hotel the traveller books personally alongside a client-provided room, is rejected outright rather than reduced to zero.** It reads as a duplicate claim for something already covered, not as an honest overestimate, and it draws the same scrutiny as any other double-claim.

If the client site genuinely has no accommodation offered and the traveller pays for a hotel themselves, that's the ordinary no-hotel client-site case, and the lodging line is claimed normally against the city's cap. The distinction is entirely about who paid, and that's the fact the approver is checking.
