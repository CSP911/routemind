# Entertainment cases — where to start

This section holds eight pages working through entertainment situations as they actually arise: a seasonal gift round, an event booked against a fixed head count, a client who shows up with extra guests, condolence and congratulation payments, and a meal that turns out to be internal after the invitation list changes.

## What this part covers
The judgement calls that sit on top of the caps and evidence rules — situations where the plain cap doesn't obviously apply, or where the head count on the day doesn't match what was booked.

## The common case
Most entertainment spend is a client meal or a gift within the ordinary cap, logged with the standard evidence, no case needed.

## Where it breaks
**The one thing worth flagging across this section is that a fixed head count is a ceiling on the claim, not a target** — extra guests who turn up on the day don't raise the per-head allowance, and the shortfall has to be absorbed or declared, not quietly averaged in.

These cases sit alongside entertainment caps and evidence, which sets the numbers this section assumes.
