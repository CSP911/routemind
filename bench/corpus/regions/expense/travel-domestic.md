# Domestic travel

Caps on fares, lodging and the daily allowance for a trip inside the country. The figures are in the
table below; this is how they are applied.

## The ordinary case
Book standard-class rail and a hotel under the nightly cap, keep the receipts, claim the allowance for
each confirmed day. Nothing needs approval beyond the trip request itself.

## Where it stops being ordinary
- **Air travel.** Only on legs over two hours. A domestic flight taken to save an afternoon is not
  reimbursed at air fare unless that leg qualifies or it was approved in advance.
- **Lodging over the cap.** Seoul and Busan have a higher cap than elsewhere; above that needs advance
  approval with a reason — a conference hotel, or a city with nothing available under the cap on the
  night in question.
- **A same-day trip.** Pays the allowance and fares only. There is no lodging to claim and the
  allowance is not prorated for a short day.
- **Your own car.** Paid per kilometre, and the per-kilometre rate replaces fuel, tolls and parking
  rather than being added to them. Record the start point, the end point and the distance — an
  odometer figure alone is not enough.

## What the allowance covers
Meals and local transport. It is paid whether or not you spent it, and it is not topped up when you
spent more. A taxi across a city is inside the allowance; a taxi because a meeting overran and the last
train had gone is the kind of thing to raise with the desk rather than assume.

Attendance confirms the days before any of this is calculated.
