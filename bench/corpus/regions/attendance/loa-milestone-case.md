# Case: a leave of absence that spans a 3/5/7-year milestone

An employee's 5-year anniversary falls in month 7 of a 12-month leave of absence. The leave of absence rule is unambiguous: 12 months on leave still counts as +1 service year, so the anniversary date itself is unaffected by being on leave.

## What actually moves and what doesn't

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| 12 months on leave of absence | +1 year (unaffected) | Prorated down for time worked |
| 3 years and over | 15 days, +1 every 2 years (capped at 25) |

The service year ticks over on the anniversary date regardless of leave, so entitlement rises from 15 to 16 days from that date. But the accrual for the year as a whole is still scaled down to the months actually worked — the milestone bump changes the daily rate, not the fact that most of the year was unworked.

## The trap

**People on leave assume the milestone is "paused" along with everything else and are surprised the higher rate applied from the anniversary date on return.** It was never paused — only the accrual volume was reduced.

That interaction between the anniversary date and the prorated total is the same one covered for accrual timing generally.
