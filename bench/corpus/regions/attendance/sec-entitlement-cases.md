# Entitlement cases

This part of Attendance & leave covers situations where the number of days someone is owed doesn't fall out of the standard service-length table on its own — a transfer in from a group company, a rehire after a gap, service that continues across a leave of absence, the year a 3/5/7-year increase lands, and the handful of situations around someone joining or leaving mid-year. Each case exists because a plain reading of the table gives the wrong answer, or no answer at all.

## The two groups below

One group works through cases where service history itself is in question — what counts as continuous, what a transfer or a rehire carries forward, and where a milestone year falls when the history isn't a clean run of full years. The other works through what happens once entitlement is set: mid-year proration, the final balance when someone leaves, and what happens if the case is never filed.

## What goes wrong

**The trap is applying the standard table before checking which case applies, rather than the other way round.** The table is right for the ordinary joiner; every case below exists precisely because someone isn't one, and running the ordinary calculation first tends to produce a number that then has to be unwound.

The booking side of Attendance & leave has the matching case set — read that once the day count itself is settled.
