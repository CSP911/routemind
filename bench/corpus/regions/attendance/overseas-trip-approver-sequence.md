# Approval order for an overseas trip

A domestic trip clears with one signature. An overseas trip needs two, and they have to land in a fixed order.

## Two approvers, one order

| Trip type | Approver(s) | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic | Line manager | Single step |
| Overseas | Line manager, then HR desk | Line manager first, HR desk second |

The line manager is judging whether the trip makes sense for the team and the workload. The HR desk is checking something different — insurance coverage abroad, visa status, and whether the destination is on any restricted list. That second check only means anything once the first has already happened.

## What happens out of sequence

**An HR desk sign-off recorded before the line manager has approved does not count — the system discards it, and the request sits waiting on the line manager step exactly as if the HR desk had never touched it.** This trips up travellers who ask HR to "get ahead of it" while the line manager is still deciding; the trip gains nothing and loses the days spent waiting.

Both signatures still have to land inside the same approval window that applies to any overseas request — a stalled first step doesn't buy the second one extra time.
