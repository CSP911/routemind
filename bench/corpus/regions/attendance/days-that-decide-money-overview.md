# Days that decide money

Most attendance entries just record where someone was. A small number of them also decide what lands on a payslip. Four of these come up often enough to need their own reference.

## The four cases

- A **business trip day** draws a flat daily allowance on top of salary, worked out separately from the hours-coding rules that cover the trip itself.
- A **leave day taken in someone's leaving month** is deducted from the balance as normal, but does not change the official last day of employment.
- **Unused leave paid out** at departure uses a wage-based daily rate, not the leave day's face value.
- A **prorated joining month** affects the first payslip's base salary by calendar days worked, separately from the prorated leave entitlement it also creates.

## Where each one is decided

Each case has its own rate table or worked example. The common trap is assuming all four follow the same payroll cutoff — they don't. The trip allowance follows the trip settlement handoff; the other three follow the leaver or new-joiner payroll timelines instead.

**Checking only the leave balance before signing off a resignation misses the allowance and the prorated salary sitting in separate calculations.**
