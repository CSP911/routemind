# Mid-year joiner cases — where to start

This section holds the case set for anyone whose service record does not start on 1 January. It covers a joiner treated by joining month, a joiner who had a leave of absence before their first working day, a joiner who transferred in from elsewhere in the group, what happens to a prorated balance at the first 1 January the company sees them, and how days accrued along the way are counted.

## The common case
Most joiners fall into the plain joining-month case: no prior leave of absence, no transfer, just a start date that lands somewhere other than 1 January. That case sets the pattern the others deviate from, and it is the one worth reading first.

## Where the other cases diverge
A leave of absence before day one changes when accrual actually starts. A transfer-in usually carries prior service forward instead of starting fresh. Both change the number that gets confirmed at the first 1 January — they don't change the shape of the confirmation itself.

**The trap**: transfer-in is routinely processed as a fresh join because the joining-month form doesn't ask about prior service, silently dropping days the joiner is entitled to keep.

Once a mid-year balance is settled, everyday tracking — hours, overtime, leave requests — runs on the same rules as for anyone hired on 1 January.
