# Days that create a tax question

## Why a day count matters
Most countries treat a foreign worker as a tax resident once they have been physically present and working there for a set stretch of time, commonly 183 days within a 12-month period. Once that line is crossed, local tax withholding and filing obligations can apply on top of, or instead of, the Korean arrangement — a question payroll and the HR desk need to get ahead of, not discover after the fact.

## The thresholds this company tracks

| Cumulative days in one country (rolling 12 months) | What happens |
|---|---|
| Under 150 | Tracked, no action needed |
| 150–182 | HR desk reviews before approving any further stint in that country |
| 183 or more | Case referred for a local tax residency assessment before the next stint is approved |

**The count is cumulative across every stint in that country, not reset by each separate approval.** Three 70-day stints in the same country over a year cross 183 just as one long stint would, and each new request is checked against the running total, not treated as its own fresh 90-day allowance.

The threshold review sits with the HR desk, the same office that signs off once cumulative days pass 90.
