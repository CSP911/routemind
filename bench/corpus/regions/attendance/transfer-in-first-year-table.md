# First-year days for a transfer-in joiner

An employee who transfers in from a group affiliate keeps their original service date, so the ordinary joining-month table does not apply to them — that table is built for someone starting a service clock from zero.

## Why the joining-month table is wrong here

The joining-month table reads a start date and hands back a prorated slice of the flat 15-day first-year entitlement. A transfer-in joiner already has years of service behind them, so their entitlement is read off the standard ladder — 15 days at 3 years and over, plus one day every two years to the cap of 25 — and then prorated only for the months remaining in the calendar year at the new entity.

## Worked figures

| Preserved service | Annual entitlement | Months remaining | First-year days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 years | 16 days | 6 of 12 | 8 days |
| 7 years | 17 days | 9 of 12 | 12.75 days, rounded per the half/quarter-day rule |

**Looking up a transfer-in joiner's start month in the joining-month table gives a lower number than they are actually owed — the table assumes no prior service, and a transfer-in joiner has some.** Use the ladder entitlement and prorate that instead.

The evidence that establishes the preserved service date is the same file HR needs on hand before this table can be used at all.
