# Salary for a partial first month

A new joiner's first payslip is not the full monthly base salary. It is prorated by the number of calendar days actually worked in that month, calculated separately from the prorated leave entitlement the same join date also creates.

## The formula

| Element | Basis |
|---|---|
| Days worked | Join date through end of that calendar month, inclusive |
| Days in month | Total calendar days in that specific month |
| First payslip | Monthly base salary × (days worked ÷ days in month) |

## The trap

**The proration uses calendar days in that specific month, not a fixed 30-day assumption.** Joining on the 15th of a 31-day month and joining on the 15th of a 28-day month produce different fractions of the monthly salary, even though the day-of-month is identical. A joiner comparing their first payslip to a colleague who started on the same day-of-month in a different month should expect a different number, not a payroll error.

This proration runs independently of the joining-month leave day count, which follows its own rounding rule.
