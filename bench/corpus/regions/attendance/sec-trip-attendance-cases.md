# Trip attendance cases — where to start

This section covers a weekend trip, an overseas trip that crosses dates against the local calendar, a trip cancelled after it was already approved, a trip taken without ever filing a request, and how a trip's attendance is reconciled once it's done.

## The common case
A trip filed in advance, taken as booked, closed out on the dates requested — attendance follows the request without adjustment. Most of the case set exists for the trips that don't follow that pattern.

## Where the pattern breaks
A weekend trip and a date-crossing overseas trip both raise the same question — which calendar day counts as attended — just for different reasons. A cancellation after approval needs the attendance record unwound explicitly; it doesn't disappear on its own once the trip doesn't happen.

**The trap**: a trip taken without a request filed is sometimes assumed to be covered retroactively by the traveller's calendar entry. It isn't — attendance for an unrequested trip has to be reconstructed and signed off separately, and it can be rejected.

Once attendance on a trip is settled, the same dates feed straight into whatever overtime or per-diem claim the trip generated.
