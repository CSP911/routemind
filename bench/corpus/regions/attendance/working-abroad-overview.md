# Working abroad

## What counts as working abroad
Working abroad is a temporary arrangement where an employee continues normal duties from a country other than Korea, for reasons unrelated to a business trip — a family posting, a partner's relocation, or a stretch of remote work agreed in advance. It is not a holiday extension and it is not a business trip: no daily allowance applies, and hours are logged the same way a domestic remote day is.

## What each piece covers
Approval follows its own chain, separate from the trip approval sequence — see the approval role and procedure for this subject. The stint itself is capped and tracked against a cumulative day count, because working from most countries long enough creates a local tax residency question that has nothing to do with Korean payroll. That threshold, and what HR does as it approaches, is covered on its own page.

**A working-abroad arrangement is not covered by the overseas trip rules, even though both involve leaving Korea** — trip approval, trip allowance and dateline handling don't apply here, and treating this as a long trip is the most common way the paperwork goes wrong.

The next stop for anyone actually planning a stint is the approval procedure, not the trip request form.
