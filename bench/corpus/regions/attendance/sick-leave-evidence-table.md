# Evidence required for sick leave

## The thresholds

| Absence length | Evidence required | How it's coded |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 consecutive days | None | Deducted from the annual leave balance |
| 3–5 consecutive days | Doctor's certificate, filed within 5 days of returning | Unpaid sick leave, separate from annual leave |
| 6 days or more | Certificate plus HR desk review | Unpaid sick leave; the long-term illness day count starts from day 1 of this absence |

## What the certificate actually changes

Filing evidence doesn't just document the absence — it moves the days out of the annual leave balance entirely from day 3 onward. Without it, every day of the absence is drawn from annual leave, however long it runs.

**A certificate filed after the 5-day window does not retroactively reclassify days already deducted from annual leave — the balance stays as first recorded, and only days from the filing date forward are corrected.** Employees who assume a late note fixes the whole absence are routinely disappointed.

The same day count that starts here is what the long-term illness conversion rule tracks toward its own threshold.
