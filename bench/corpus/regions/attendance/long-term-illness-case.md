# A worked example: sick leave crossing 90 days

## The timeline

An employee falls ill on 1 March. A certificate is filed on 5 March covering the absence from day 3 onward, so 1–2 March are deducted from annual leave and everything from 3 March is coded as unpaid sick leave. The illness does not resolve.

| Date | Day count | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 March | Days 1–2 | Deducted from annual leave |
| 3 March – 28 May | Days 3–90 | Unpaid sick leave |
| 29 May onward | Day 91+ | Converted to unpaid leave of absence |

## What HR does

Nothing further needs filing to trigger the conversion — HR updates the coding in the attendance system on day 91 based on the certificate already on file. The employee's service date and any upcoming milestone year continue to move exactly as they would on ordinary leave of absence.

## The trap

**No new certificate resets the 90-day count.** A second doctor's note covering the same continuous illness doesn't restart the clock or delay the conversion — only a genuine return to work followed by a new absence would begin a fresh count.
