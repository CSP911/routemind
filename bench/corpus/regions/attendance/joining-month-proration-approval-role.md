# Who confirms a new joiner's prorated salary

A join date that falls mid-month triggers two separate proration calculations, run by two separate teams. Confusing one for the other is the most common reason a new joiner's first month looks wrong on paper when it isn't.

## The two confirmations

| What | Who confirms it | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Prorated first salary | Payroll team | Calendar days actually worked in the joining month |
| Prorated leave day balance | HR desk | Joining-month table, confirmed within 14 days of join date |

## Why the split matters

The salary figure is fixed once payroll closes the month and cannot be revisited without a correction request. The leave day balance is confirmed on its own 14-day clock and can still be sitting open when the first payslip has already gone out.

**Assuming that a correct first payslip means the leave balance has also been confirmed — or the reverse — is the trap: the two run independently, and one being right says nothing about the other.**

Anyone still waiting on the leave day confirmation past that 14-day mark should raise it through the same channel used for a misrecorded join date, not through payroll.
