# The rate for unused leave payout

Each unused leave day is paid out at a daily rate calculated from wages, not simply looked up from the current base salary.

## How the rate is built

| Basis | Period used |
|---|---|
| Average daily wage | Last 3 months of ordinary wages before the last day of employment |
| Included | Base salary, fixed allowances |
| Excluded | One-off bonuses, irregular overtime pay from a single month |

The average daily wage is multiplied by the number of unused leave days to reach the payout amount.

## The trap

**Using the 3-month average means a recent raise barely moves the payout, and a one-off bonus paid in that window can distort it either way if it isn't correctly excluded.** An employee who negotiated a salary increase two months before leaving will see a payout closer to their old rate than their new one, because most of the 3-month window still reflects the earlier salary.

Whether that payout reaches the final payslip or the one after depends on when the balance is confirmed, covered next.
