# A mid-year joiner who went on leave of absence

An employee joined on 1 April 2023 and worked six months before starting an approved leave of absence on 1 October 2023, returning on 1 April 2024. The leave of absence spans the first 1 January the employee would otherwise reset against.

## Working it through

| Period | Status | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Apr–Sep 2023 (6 months) | Actively worked | Prorated days accrued per the joining-month table |
| Oct 2023–Mar 2024 (6 months) | Leave of absence | Service clock keeps running; no new accrual |
| 1 Apr 2024 | 1-year anniversary reached | Entitlement question: full 15 days, or prorated? |

The service clock treats the LOA months as continuous service, so the employee does reach their 1-year anniversary on schedule. But per the leave-of-absence accrual rule, the entitlement granted at that anniversary is **prorated down for the six months actually worked, not the full 15 days a joiner who worked the whole year would get.**

This is the same principle applied in the milestone-year LOA case, just landing on a first-anniversary date instead of a 3/5/7-year one — the anniversary date is unaffected by the leave, only the day count attached to it.
