# Which mid-year joiner situation applies

"Started partway through the year" covers three different mechanics, and applying the wrong one produces a wrong balance that looks plausible.

## The three shapes

- **Straight joiner** — someone new to the company. Their first-year balance comes off the joining-month proration table, and it converts to a full annual entitlement at the first 1 January.
- **Joiner who was on leave of absence** — a straight joiner whose first year includes a period of leave of absence. The months actually worked are prorated as usual; the LOA months add no further accrual but do not shrink what was already earned.
- **Transfer-in joiner** — someone moving from a group affiliate with a documented, evidenced transfer. Their balance is never read from the proration table at all.

## Which document to use

| Situation | Governing document |
|---|---|
| New joiner, no leave of absence | proration-table |
| New joiner whose first year includes LOA | mid-year-joiner-loa-case |
| Transfer-in with evidence on file | mid-year-joiner-transfer-case |

**A transferred employee is not a joining-month case just because the start date falls mid-year.** Reading their entitlement off the proration table is the mistake this index exists to prevent.

Evidence for the transfer itself is checked separately — see the entitlement case evidence checklist.
