# Transfer vs rehire: same gap, different outcome

## What actually distinguishes them
| | Group transfer | Rehire |
|---|---|---|
| Employment contract | Continues, moves to new entity | Ends, then a new one starts |
| Gap on paper | None, even if the move takes weeks | Exists, even if short |
| Service date | Preserved | Restarts, unless within a continuity window |
| Accrual carried over | Yes | No |

## The trap
**A same-day move between two group companies is not automatically a transfer — it's a transfer only if the contract is handed off without a termination in between.** If the employee resigns from company A and signs a fresh contract with company B, even the same afternoon, that is legally a rehire, not a transfer, and the accrual ladder resets to first-year regardless of how short the gap looks on a calendar.

This is why the paperwork matters more than the timeline: check which one was actually filed before assuming continuity, and see the rehire gap thresholds for how a genuine break in service is measured.
