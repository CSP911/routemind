# Mid-year joiner

Someone who joined part-way through a year has two different entitlements in their first eighteen
months, and the order they arrive in is what confuses people. The exact figure by joining month is in
the proration table below.

## What happens, in order
1. **From the joining date.** One day accrues for each completed month, up to eleven. These are real
   days — they can be booked as soon as they accrue.
2. **On the first 1 January after joining.** A prorated grant arrives, calculated from how much of the
   year remained when you joined. The monthly accrual stops here.
3. **On the second 1 January.** The ordinary fifteen days, the same as everybody else.

## What is *not* true
- The two are **not added together.** The prorated grant replaces the monthly accrual; it does not
  come on top of it.
- The proration is **not** worked from the anniversary. It is worked from the joining month to the
  31st of December, which is why joining in December gives so few days.
- Days accrued monthly and left unused before the first 1 January **lapse** like any others.

## Worked example
Joined 3 July. By 31 December five days have accrued (August through December). On 1 January eight
days arrive — the prorated grant for a July joiner. Any of the five that were not used are gone. The
following 1 January, fifteen.

Every year a handful of cases fall between these — a joiner who was on leave of absence, or who
transferred from a group company. Those are for the HR desk, and they are decided case by case.
