# A trip approved after it left

An employee's overseas trip request sat in draft for 6 days and was submitted only the morning of departure — well past the 5-working-day approval deadline for overseas travel, and with no time left for the line manager and HR desk to both act.

## What happened
The employee travelled without an approved request. On day 2 of the trip, they filed the trip approval exception form along with the original request details, inside the 3-calendar-day window measured from departure.

## What the review found
The HR desk approved the exception, since the form was filed in time and the trip details matched the itinerary. The attendance record was entered as exception-approved rather than as an ordinary overseas trip, and the days were coded as trip days once approval cleared.

## What it changed
The trip itself was recognised without further dispute. The daily allowance, however, still had to be claimed separately, and because the exception approval landed close to month-end, the claim risked slipping to the following month's payslip if not filed promptly.

Whether a claim like that lands on this month's payslip or the next one is governed entirely by the allowance claim deadline, not by how the trip itself was approved.
