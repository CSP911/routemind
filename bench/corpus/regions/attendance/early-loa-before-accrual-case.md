# A first-year joiner starting leave of absence before their first day accrues

An employee joins on 1 March and is approved for leave of absence starting 20 March, before a full month of service has been completed.

## What has and hasn't happened

The service date is fixed at 1 March regardless of what follows — the joining-month rounding rule and the service clock both start from the actual join date. But the joining-month table prorates entitlement by completed months worked, and this employee has not completed one. No day has posted to their balance yet.

## What continues during the leave of absence

| Period | Status | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1–19 March | Actively worked | No completed month, no accrual yet |
| From 20 March | Leave of absence | Service clock keeps running; no new accrual |

**The instinct is to treat "no days accrued yet" as a sign the join date itself is in question — it isn't.** The service date stands from 1 March; only the first prorated day is delayed until a full month has actually been worked, whether before or after the leave.

Once the employee returns, proration resumes exactly where the joining-month table would have left off had no leave intervened.

This is the same clock that governs when a first-year joiner going on leave of absence must tell HR before their prorated balance lapses.
