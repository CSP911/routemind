# Entitlement cases: how service history changes the count

These twelve cases work through what counts as service when the history isn't a single unbroken run at one employer — a transfer in from a group affiliate, a rehire after a gap, a leave of absence that sits inside an otherwise continuous run, and the year a 3, 5 or 7-year increase should land when one of those events falls close to the milestone. The join date recorded in the system drives all of them, so a handful of these cases are really about getting that date right in the first place.

## The common case

Most transfers and rehires resolve by checking whether the gap or the move counts as continuity-preserving under the leave-of-absence rules; if it does, service carries forward and the milestone year moves with it. If it doesn't, the clock resets at the new join date, however short the gap looks in practice.

## What goes wrong

**The trap is assuming a transfer and a rehire are treated the same because the paperwork looks similar.** They aren't — one typically preserves service, the other typically doesn't — and the difference changes which milestone year applies. Confirm which case actually applies before quoting a day count to anyone.

Once service history is settled, the other half of this set covers what the resulting entitlement actually pays out mid-year.
