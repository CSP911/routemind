# An overseas trip that crosses dates

An employee departs Incheon at 11:00 PM on a Tuesday and lands in the destination city at 6:00 AM local time, which is still Tuesday evening in Korea. The trip itself, and any duty performed on arrival, is recorded against Korean calendar dates throughout — not the local date at the destination.

## Why this matters

| Reference point | Used for attendance? |
|---|---|
| Korean calendar date | Yes — always |
| Destination local date | No |
| Departure/arrival local time | No |

**The trap is entering the trip against the destination's local date because that's what the boarding pass and hotel folio show — the attendance record has to use Korean dates for every day of the trip, including the return leg.** Someone converting a five-day itinerary literally, date by date, from a foreign itinerary will usually enter one day wrong at either end.

Once the correct Korean dates are set, whether a given day falls on a weekend follows the same rule as any other trip.
