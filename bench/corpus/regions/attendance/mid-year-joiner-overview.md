# Mid-year joiner cases

Anyone who joins after 1 January accrues leave on a different clock from someone who started at the beginning of the year. Three things make this more than a simple pro-rata sum: the joining month itself, whether the person was on leave of absence during their first year, and whether they transferred in with prior service already recognised.

## Where the base numbers come from

The number of days a new joiner accrues before their first 31 December is set by the joining-month proration table (see the separate table document). This page covers what happens around that number, not the number itself.

## The complications

- **A joiner on leave of absence during their first year** does not accrue for the LOA months, even though their service clock keeps running.
- **A joiner who transferred in** from a group affiliate may already have service that skips the proration table entirely.
- **The first 1 January after joining** is a hard boundary: accrued days earned in the first partial year do not automatically carry into the next.

Each of these is worked through in its own case below. None of them is optional — a joiner who falls into more than one at once (transferred in, then immediately on leave of absence) is handled by combining the two, not by picking whichever gives fewer days.

The lapse mechanics at that first 1 January are the part people get wrong most often — see the next page.
