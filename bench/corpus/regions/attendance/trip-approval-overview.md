# Getting a trip approved before it starts

A business trip moves from a draft, to a submitted request, to an approved entry in the attendance system. Departing before all three stages clear is what turns an ordinary trip into an exception case.

## The three stages
A trip begins as a draft in the trip request form — dates and destination entered, nothing sent yet. Submitting it starts the approval clock. Only an approved request creates the attendance record that a trip day is coded against; a draft or a pending request creates nothing.

## Domestic vs overseas
A domestic trip needs sign-off from the line manager alone. An overseas trip needs the line manager and the HR desk, because the HR desk also confirms insurance and allowance eligibility for travel outside Korea. **A manager's approval on an overseas trip is not the final approval** — the request stays pending until the HR desk clears it too, and a trip can show "manager approved" while still being unapproved for departure purposes.

## When it doesn't happen in time
If departure arrives before approval is complete, the trip isn't automatically invalid — it moves onto the narrower, time-limited path used for approval given after the fact.

Once a trip is approved, what its hours actually count as follows the ordinary attendance coding rules, not this one.
