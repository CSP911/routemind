# Taking time off instead of overtime pay

An employee submitting an overtime request can choose to bank the hours as time off instead of receiving the premium pay for them. The choice is made on the request itself, alongside the usual date, hours and reason.

## How the banking works

| Overtime type | Banked as |
|---|---|
| Ordinary overtime | 1 hour worked = 1 hour banked |
| Night or holiday overtime | 1 hour worked = 1.5 hours banked |

Banked hours are drawn from the attendance system in the same way half-day leave is, and can be taken in blocks agreed with the line manager.

## Using the balance

Banked hours must be used within 60 days of the date they were worked. Unused hours are not paid out at the 60-day mark — they simply lapse.

**The choice between pay and time off is fixed at the moment the request is submitted, not after the hours are worked.** A manager who lets someone work first and decide later has approved neither a valid overtime request nor a valid banking request — the hours fall back to the unrequested-overtime treatment.

Where the banked balance interacts with a resignation before it is used is not covered here — see the final leave settlement rule instead.
