# Trip attendance outcomes at a glance

Five trip situations don't follow the general attendance rule cleanly, and each has its own worked case elsewhere in this area. This page is the fast lookup once you already know which case applies.

## What actually gets recorded

| Situation | Hours recorded as | Record lands in |
|---|---|---|
| Weekend trip | Actual hours worked, not a standard day | The pay period the work fell in |
| Overseas trip crossing dates | The Korean calendar date, not the local date | The date the trip started in Korea |
| Cancelled after approval | Reversed in full once the trip is called off | Corrected before the next payslip cutoff, or the one after |
| No request filed before departure | Unauthorized absence until a manager retroactively approves it | Held pending approval, not backfilled automatically |
| Spans the month close | Split across the two payroll periods it touches | Each month gets only the days that fall inside it |

**A trip without a filed request is never assumed to be business travel — silence before departure defaults to absence, not approval.** Retroactive sign-off can fix the record, but only once a manager actually reviews it; nobody backfills it overnight.

Once the trip itself is settled in the attendance system, the same days become the starting point for the expense claim.
