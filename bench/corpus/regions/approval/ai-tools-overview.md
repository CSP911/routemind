# Use of AI tools

A tool is "approved" only when it is on the current list held by IT — not when a team has quietly been using it for months without anyone objecting. The list distinguishes tools with a company enterprise agreement, which do not retain input for training, from the consumer or free tier of the same product, which usually does.

## What may not go into one

Personal data, contract terms before signing, and unpublished financial figures may not be entered into any tool that has not been confirmed as non-training by IT.

**The trap is that a paid subscription is not the same guarantee as an enterprise agreement.** A team lead's own card can buy the consumer tier of a tool already approved at enterprise level, and the two handle the same input differently — one keeps it, one does not.

## Adding a new tool

A tool not on the list is proposed the same way a purchase or policy change is: drafted, routed to IT and Legal for consent, and approved by whoever's band the subscription cost falls under. A tool that will touch personal data needs Legal's consent regardless of what it costs.

The list of what is currently approved, and what counts as restricted input, sit next to this as their own pages.
