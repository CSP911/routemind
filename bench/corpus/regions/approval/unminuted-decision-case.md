# A decision with no minute

A division head approves an over-cap expense in a regular team meeting — within their band, no objection from anyone present, nothing unusual about the amount. No minute is drafted, because nobody treated the meeting as one that needed one.

## What happens later

Six months on, an audit reviewing over-cap expenses finds the spend in the ledger but no minute, no draft referencing a decision, and no sign-off on file. The division head remembers approving it. Nothing in the file confirms that.

## Why it counts as unapproved

The authority to approve the spend was there. **What was missing is proof the authority was exercised, and a remembered approval carries no more weight on audit than no approval at all.** The expense is treated the same as one nobody ever signed off, regardless of whether the division head's account is accurate.

## What should have happened

The moment the division head said "approved" in the meeting, that meeting needed a minute — signed by the division head, filed within the window, retained for as long as the spend draft itself.

This is the same gap as a signed chain with no consent behind it: a clean-looking outcome with nothing underneath it to check.
