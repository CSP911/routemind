# Quotes from vendors under common ownership

A purchase draft for 2,900,000 KRW arrived with three quotes attached, satisfying the count for its band. Finance, asked to consent on budget grounds, noticed two of the three vendors listed the same registered address and filed under the same corporate group.

## Why the count didn't help

Three quotes are meant to show the price was tested against the market. Two quotes from companies under one parent are effectively one price with a second letterhead. The draft technically satisfied the quote count table and still didn't satisfy what the count exists to prove.

**A consenting team checking budget lines is not automatically checking vendor independence** — that check has to be asked for explicitly, or it gets skipped in exactly the cases like this one where the quotes otherwise look fine.

## What happened next

The draft was held pending a genuine third quote, from an unrelated vendor. It came back higher, and the purchase still went through — the point was never to force a cheaper price, only to make sure the comparison was real.

Related-vendor quotes are a variant of the same problem a split purchase creates: paperwork that satisfies a rule's letter without satisfying what the rule is for.
