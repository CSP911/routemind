# Signing off a minute

Writing the minute and approving it are different jobs, and one person doing both does not make the minute valid on its own.

## Steps

1. Whoever took notes drafts the minute within the filing window.
2. It circulates to attendees for factual correction — dates, names, what was actually said.
3. The person who held the authority for the decision — team lead, division head, or CEO — reviews and signs.
4. The signed minute is filed with the records desk against the decision it records.

## Who signs

The signer is the authority that made the decision, not the chair of the meeting if those are different people, and not the drafter by default.

**The trap is letting the drafter's signature stand in for the approver's.** A minute written and signed by the same person who has no authority over the decision looks complete but proves nothing — it is the meeting equivalent of a chain with the wrong approver.
