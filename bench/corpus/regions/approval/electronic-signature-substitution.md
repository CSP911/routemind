# Electronic signature in place of a seal

An electronic signature can stand in for the company seal on documents that do not require notarisation or CEO-band authority — internal certificates, domestic filings that never leave the division. It does not stand in for the corporate seal.

## Where it applies

| Document | Electronic signature allowed | Seal required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal certificate | Yes | No |
| Domestic filing, non-notarised | Yes | No |
| Contract | No | Corporate seal |
| Overseas filing | No | Corporate seal, plus apostille |

## The trap

**An electronic signature on a contract does not become valid because a manager approved the draft electronically upstream in the approval chain.** The seal requirement sits on the document itself, at the subject level, not on how the draft was signed off before it got there. A contract that was approved electronically end to end still needs the physical corporate seal to count as a contract at all.

This is the same subject-over-value logic that puts contracts and overseas trips on the CEO's band regardless of amount.
