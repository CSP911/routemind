# Consent and the chain are separate checks

A consenting team confirms that a draft is sound on its own terms — Finance on budget, Legal on liability, HR on headcount. The chain confirms that the right person signed at the right authority level. Neither one stands in for the other, and a draft can fail on either side independently of the other.

## What each side checks

| Check | Who does it | What it confirms |
|---|---|---|
| Consent | Finance, Legal, HR, depending on draft kind | The draft is sound on its own terms |
| Chain | Drafter, reviewer, approver | The right person signed at the right authority |

## When one side fails without the other

An objection from a consenting team stops a draft before it ever reaches the approver — a 2,600,000 KRW equipment purchase held at Finance over a missing budget line never gets to the division head's desk until that objection is resolved.

A missing consent is worse, because it surfaces later: a contract signed by the CEO and filed can still fail an audit six months on if Legal's sign-off was never logged, even though the signature itself was at the correct level.

**A correctly signed chain does not prove consent happened, and a clean consent record does not prove the chain was correct** — a 3,200,000 KRW purchase signed by a division head instead of the CEO is void on authority grounds no matter how clean Finance's consent file is.

Which team consents to which draft kind is fixed on the kind, not negotiated file by file.
