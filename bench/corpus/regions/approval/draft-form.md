# Draft document form

One sheet, five fields and an attachment list. The exact wording each field expects is in the field
table below this page; this is what the form is for and how it flows.

## The shape of it
The top half is identification — title, date, drafter, the chain. The bottom half is the proposal
itself: purpose, authority, amount, expected effect. The attachments sit under that and travel with
the document for as long as it is kept.

## How it moves
1. You complete it and route it to the chain you built.
2. The reviewer checks the content is right and sends it on.
3. Each consenting team either agrees or objects. **An objection stops it there** — it does not go to
   the approver with an unresolved objection attached.
4. The approver decides. On approval it is numbered and filed automatically.

## Editing after it has moved
Once routed, the fields are locked. A correction means withdrawing it and refiling, which is
deliberate: a document that could be edited after somebody approved it would not be evidence of
anything. Small corrections before the first step are free — withdraw, fix, route again.

## The parts people leave blank
**Authority** and **expected effect**. The first is required always; the second above 3,000,000 KRW.
Both are the fields an auditor reads first, and a draft missing them will be asked about long after
everyone has forgotten the details.
