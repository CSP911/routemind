# Retention holds during an audit

A document that reaches the end of its retention period is not automatically clear to destroy if it sits inside an active audit — tax, labor, or internal.

## What a hold does
Once a document is flagged for an audit, its destruction date is suspended even if the retention table says the period matured months ago. Tax invoices at their five-year mark stay in place until the audit that touches them closes, however long that takes.

## Who can place one
A hold is placed by Legal or Finance, whichever function owns the audit, and lifted by the same function once it closes. The records desk does not decide whether a document is under audit — it only acts on the hold once notified.

## The trap
**A matured retention date is not evidence a document is safe to destroy.** The January destruction list is built from retention dates alone unless someone checks it against open holds first — a document can look overdue for destruction and be sitting inside a live audit at the same time.

That check is exactly the step the January list depends on before anything is signed off.
