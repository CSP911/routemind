# Minute retention

A minute has no retention period of its own. It is kept for as long as the decision it documents.

## How it maps

| Decision recorded | Minute retained |
|---|---|
| Contract signing | 10 years |
| Purchase or hire draft | 5 years |
| Policy change | 5 years |
| General discussion with no band decision | 3 years |

This mirrors how the underlying draft is retained, and the clock starts from the meeting date, the same way a draft's clock starts from signing or filing rather than from when it takes effect.

## Mixed meetings

A single meeting sometimes produces more than one decision — a purchase approval and a policy discussion in the same sitting. The minute is filed once, and the whole minute is retained for the longest period any of its decisions requires.

**The trap is splitting one minute into shorter-lived fragments to match each decision.** A minute is a single record; keeping only the "purchase" portion for five years and letting the rest lapse at three destroys evidence of decisions that are still supposed to be on file.
