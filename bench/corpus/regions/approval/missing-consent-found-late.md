# A missing consent found after approval

A 4,000,000 KRW service contract goes through drafting, is signed by the CEO as contracts always require, and is filed for its ten-year retention period. A routine file check six months later finds no record of Legal's consent, which the contract's subject matter required.

## What the CEO's signature does not fix

The CEO's approval covers the authority to sign a contract of that size. It does not substitute for Legal's consent on the terms, because the two checks answer different questions — one is "is this within authority," the other is "does Legal have an objection to the terms." A signed contract with a missing consent is not automatically void, but it is not properly closed either.

## What has to happen

Legal reviews the contract as it stands now, not as a formality. If they raise no objection, their consent is filed retroactively with a note explaining the gap. If they do object, the contract has to be renegotiated or the gap escalated — the CEO's earlier signature does not settle that.

**Finding the gap does not start a fresh retention clock — the original ten years still runs from the contract's effective date.**

The same pattern shows up on the approver's side too: the right signature, but the wrong person behind it.
