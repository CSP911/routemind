# An advance that didn't match the receipts

A team lead requests and receives a 450,000 KRW advance for a two-day domestic trip, correctly within their own band. The trip runs shorter than planned.

## The reconciliation

Five business days after return, the team lead files receipts totalling 380,000 KRW. The form is submitted on time, so the reconciliation deadline is met — but the remaining 70,000 KRW is never returned to the company and no one flags it.

## The finding

An internal audit three months later turns up the gap. No one had treated the unreturned balance as its own item; it had simply been filed under "reconciled" alongside the receipts.

## The trap

**A reconciliation filed on time is not the same as an advance closed correctly.** Meeting the deadline only confirms receipts were submitted — it says nothing about whether the full amount was accounted for. Any difference between what was advanced and what was spent has to be returned or explained, or the advance stays open on record no matter how promptly the paperwork went in.

The same gap, left long enough, is exactly what a later audit is built to catch.
