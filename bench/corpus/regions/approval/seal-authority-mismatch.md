# Case: a company seal on an overseas filing

A division head authorised the company seal on a supply agreement bound for a partner's bank in Vietnam, treating it as a routine domestic-style filing because the value involved was well under the division head's own spend band.

## What happened

The receiving bank rejected the filing outright: the document required the corporate seal, since anything crossing the CEO's overseas-trip and contract band is decided by subject, not by cost, and the company seal carries no authority over either subject. The draft was pulled back, resealed with the corporate seal under the CEO's authority, fitted with a fresh seal certificate, and refiled with an apostille attached before the bank would accept it.

## Why it happened

The division head read the transaction as low-value and assumed low-value meant company-seal territory. **Value never enters the decision here — a contract or an overseas filing needs the corporate seal at any amount, and no division head band covers either subject.**

The delay this caused mirrors what happens when a purchase draft is filed with the wrong approver in the chain: the paperwork looks complete, but the signature underneath it does not count.
