# Logging a destruction

Destroying a document removes the only direct evidence that it existed and was handled correctly. The log entry is what stands in its place.

## What each entry records
- The document's kind and title
- The retention basis it was held under, statute or company policy, and the number of years
- The date it was created or signed, and the date its retention period matured
- The date of destruction and who authorised it
- Whether it came off a January list or was destroyed early, and why

## Where the log lives
The records desk keeps the log separately from the documents it describes, so a destroyed contract or invoice still leaves a trace of when it was held and when it left.

## The trap
**A destruction without a log entry is indistinguishable from a document that was never filed at all.** If an auditor asks years later why a tax invoice cannot be produced, the log is the only thing that shows it was destroyed on schedule rather than lost, misplaced, or removed early.

That distinction matters most for the kinds most likely to be checked later — tax records and contracts — where the periods run longest.
