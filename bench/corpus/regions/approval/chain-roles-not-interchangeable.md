# Roles in the chain are not interchangeable

A chain has four kinds of participant, and each one is checking something the others aren't. Doubling one person up across two roles doesn't save a step — it removes a check.

## The four roles

| Role | What they check |
|---|---|
| Drafter | Writes the draft, states authority and amount |
| Reviewer | Checks the draft is complete before it reaches the approver |
| Approver | Signs off within their authority band |
| Consenting team | Confirms no objection from the part of the business the draft touches |

## Where this goes wrong

A team lead drafts a purchase for their own team and also reviews it before sending it up — nothing catches an error in the draft, because the same person who wrote it is the one who was supposed to catch problems in it. Consenting teams have the same issue: a division head cannot consent on behalf of a team they also approved the spend for.

**The reviewer and the drafter can be different people on paper while actually being the same person functionally — a manager rewriting their own draft and then "reviewing" it has not added a check.**

The same separation is why the chain itself — who drafts, who reviews, who approves — is worth getting right from the start.
