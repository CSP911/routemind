# Meetings and minutes

Most meetings are never minuted. A minute exists to prove that a decision was made, by whom, under what authority — not to summarise a conversation.

## When a minute is required

Any meeting that produces a decision inside an authority band — a spend approval, a hire, a policy change, an over-cap expense — needs a minute. A meeting that only discusses options, with no decision reached, does not.

## What the minute has to do

It records the decision and the authority under which it was made, in enough detail that someone reading it six months later, without having been in the room, can tell what happened and who is accountable for it.

## Where it goes

Once signed off, the minute is filed with the records desk under the retention period tied to the decision it records — not a fixed clock of its own.

**The trap is treating "we didn't plan to decide anything" as a reason to skip the minute.** If the discussion ends in an actual decision, the minute is required regardless of what the meeting was scheduled to be.
