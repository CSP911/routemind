# Sealing a document for use overseas

A document leaving the country carries the same authority requirement as any other CEO-band subject: overseas matters are decided by the CEO regardless of cost, and the seal that goes on the document is the corporate seal, not the company seal.

## Steps

1. Confirm the draft has been routed and signed under the CEO's authority, per the destination-based rule for overseas subjects.
2. Apply the corporate seal and attach the matching seal certificate.
3. Add an apostille or consular legalisation, depending on the destination country's requirements.
4. Attach a certified translation if the receiving country's language differs from the original.

## What goes wrong

**The corporate seal makes a document valid inside Korea; it does not make it valid abroad.** An overseas filing that skips the apostille or legalisation step is rejected at the receiving end no matter how correctly the seal itself was applied — the seal and the legalisation are two separate requirements, not one.

Where the receiving country does not recognise a physical seal at all, an electronic signature is sometimes offered as an alternative, and it is worth checking that before assuming the corporate seal covers it.
