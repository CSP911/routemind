# Two orders, one purchase

A team lead needs new hardware for the team, priced at 900,000 KRW as one order. Filed as a single purchase draft, that amount sits with the division head. The team lead instead places two orders of 450,000 KRW each, on the same day, from the same vendor, both under their own 500,000 KRW limit, and signs both alone.

## Why it doesn't hold

**Splitting a purchase does not change what was actually bought.** The two orders are one purchase with one true amount, and the true amount decides the band — not the number of drafts used to describe it. When Finance or an auditor reviews the vendor's invoices later, the two orders reconstruct into the 900,000 KRW they were always going to be, and the team lead's signature covers neither.

The intent behind the split does not matter either — a genuine attempt to move faster fails the same way a deliberate one does. The fix is to file the true amount once and let it land in the band it belongs to; a division head signing 900,000 KRW takes less time than unwinding two invalid signatures later.

This same logic runs the other way across a full year, where a recurring agreement's total is what decides the band.
