# Handling a subject access request

A subject access request can arrive from a current employee, a former one, or an unsuccessful candidate, and it asks the same question regardless of source: what does the company hold, where did it come from, and who has seen it.

## Steps

1. Confirm the requester's identity before searching — do not disclose to someone whose identity has not been checked.
2. Route the request to the records desk, which searches both the active file and anything already logged toward destruction.
3. Compile what is held, its source, and any third party it was shared with.
4. Respond in writing within 10 business days of confirming identity.
5. If more time is needed, notify the requester before the window closes and extend once, by up to 10 further business days.

## Response window

| Stage | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Standard response | 10 business days from identity confirmation |
| Extension, if notified in advance | One further 10 business days |
| Extension without notice | Not available |

**The clock starts at identity confirmation, not at the date the request arrived.** A request left unconfirmed for a week does not shrink the response window, but the eventual 10 days start later than the requester assumes — confirm identity promptly, or the delay looks like the company's own.

A request that surfaces a record that should already have been destroyed under retention is a separate problem, not a reason to hold the response.
