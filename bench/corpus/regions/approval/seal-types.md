# Company seal and corporate seal

Two seals sit in the office, and they are not interchangeable. The company seal falls under the division head's authority band, alongside domestic trips and over-cap expenses. The corporate seal sits with the CEO, in the same band as contracts, hiring and dismissal, and overseas trips.

## What each one is for

| Seal | Authorised by | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company seal | Division head | Internal certificates, domestic administrative filings, routine paperwork within the division |
| Corporate seal | CEO | Contracts, overseas filings, anything the CEO band covers by subject rather than value |

## Why the split matters

The company seal is convenient precisely because it does not require CEO sign-off, and that is where the trouble starts. **A division head who authorises the company seal on a document that actually needs the corporate seal — a contract, a filing bound overseas — has not moved the draft forward, whatever stamp appears on the page.** The document still needs the CEO's seal to be valid; the company seal impression does not count toward that.

Both seals are held in the same custody arrangement, but how they are checked out and logged is a separate matter.
