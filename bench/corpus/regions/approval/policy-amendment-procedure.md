# Amending a published policy

A policy that needs a small change does not always require full withdrawal and refiling. If the update is a correction, clarification, or narrow addition that leaves the substance of the rule intact, it can move through as an amendment — a filing that names the version it changes, carries the same authority requirement as the version it amends, and takes its own place in the version history.

## When amendment applies, and when it does not

An amendment only works if the original rule stays recognisable. If the change reverses what the policy actually decides — who it binds, what it caps, which authority it requires — it is a new policy, not an amendment, and needs its own proposal and review from the start.

## Authority required

The amendment needs the same authority that originally published the policy it changes. A division head who published a policy under their own band cannot amend it into a CEO-band subject by filing it as a small update — publication authority is set by what the policy touches, not by whose desk the amendment lands on.

## What the amendment does not inherit

**An amendment that fails to name the version it changes leaves both versions looking valid at once** — the amendment reads as a fresh policy with no history, and whoever checks it later has no way to tell which one is in force.

Each amendment opens its own five-year retention clock, on top of the one already running for the version it replaces — each version keeps its own.
