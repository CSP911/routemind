# Authority at each stage of a policy's life

A policy moves through proposal, review, publication and withdrawal, and each stage needs someone with the right authority to act — not necessarily the same person throughout.

## Who acts at each stage

| Stage | Who acts | Authority required |
|---|---|---|
| Propose | Drafter | Any level — the draft states what the policy touches |
| Review | Consenting teams and approver | Matches what the policy touches |
| Publish | Approver | Same band as the subject the policy governs |
| Withdraw | Whoever holds the authority that published it | Same band as publication |

## Authority follows the subject, not the drafter

A team lead can propose a policy that turns out to touch a CEO-band subject — hiring criteria, contract terms, the corporate seal. Proposing it does not raise the team lead's own authority. The draft still routes to whichever level the subject requires, the same way a purchase draft routes by amount rather than by who wrote it up.

**Publishing authority is not transferable between stages of the same draft** — a division head who reviewed and consented to a policy cannot then publish it if the subject requires the CEO, even though their name is already on the file from an earlier stage.

Who ends up accountable for the policy once it is live is a separate question from who published it — that gets assigned on its own.
