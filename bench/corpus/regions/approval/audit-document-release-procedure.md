# Releasing documents to an audit request

Every audit request is logged before a single document moves — the requester, the date, what is being asked for, and who cleared it. The records desk pulls the file, confirms nothing on the list is already flagged for a hold, and prepares a copy rather than the original for anything under seal or under a retention period still running.

## Who clears it

The clearing authority depends on who is asking. Internal audit requests are cleared by the division head that owns the document; a request reaching into contracts, personnel records, or anything the CEO would ordinarily sign is cleared by the CEO instead. External auditor requests always go through the CEO, regardless of the amount or subject the document concerns, because the recipient sits outside the company's own chain.

## The trap

**A cleared request is not a standing arrangement.** Clearing one quarter's expense records for an external auditor does not clear the next batch they ask for a week later — each request is logged and cleared on its own, the same way a signature inside a spend band does not carry over to a purchase that crosses it.

Once the copy is made, the log entry is the only record that the original never left custody — the same log an audit itself would ask to see.
