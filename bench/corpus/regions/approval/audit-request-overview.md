# Audit requests, internal and external

An audit request can come from two different places, and the difference decides what happens next. An internal audit is run by the company's own audit function, drawing on the same authority chain as any other draft — it can ask a division head or the CEO to produce a document, and the request is answered inside the company. An external auditor works for an outside firm engaged for a statutory or contractual audit and has no standing in the company's approval chain at all. Whatever they receive, they receive because someone with authority decided to give it to them, not because they asked.

**Treating the two the same is the trap.** A manager who would hand a contract straight to internal audit sometimes hands the same contract to an external auditor on the same instinct — but an external request needs a release decision first, at the authority level the document itself would need to leave the building at all.

What goes out is a copy checked against the file, never the sealed original. A request that touches a document already flagged for retention follows the hold rules regardless of who is asking.
