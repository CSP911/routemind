# Who owns a policy after it is published

A published policy needs an owner distinct from whoever drafted it — someone who answers for it when a team asks whether it still applies, and who has to act if it needs to change.

## How ownership is assigned

| Policy subject | Owner |
|---|---|
| Spend and purchasing rules within a division | Division head |
| Anything touching CEO-band subjects — hiring, contracts, the seals | CEO |
| Cross-division practice | Whoever the publishing authority names in the proposal |

## The trap

**Ownership does not follow the drafter once the policy is published.** A team lead who wrote the original proposal has no standing to revise or withdraw it later unless the proposal itself named them as owner — that authority sits with whoever held the approval band, not whoever held the pen.

An owner who leaves the role does not take the policy with them; it is reassigned the same way delegated authority is, not carried by habit.
