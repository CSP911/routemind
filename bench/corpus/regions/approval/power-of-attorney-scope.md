# Power of attorney scope

## What it can carry
A power of attorney lets someone sign on behalf of a person who holds the authority, for a transaction that person could sign themselves. A division head can grant one covering a purchase order inside their own 3,000,000 KRW band. Nobody can grant a power of attorney for a contract or the corporate seal, because that authority sits with the CEO alone and cannot be handed down by anyone below.

## What it must state
| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Counterparty | Named, not "any party" |
| Transaction | The specific deal, not a general grant |
| Expiry | A fixed date |
| Grantor's authority | Must match the band the transaction falls in |

**A power of attorney that names no expiry and no specific transaction reads as a blanket grant, and blanket grants are not valid — they get treated as if no power of attorney was issued at all.** A drafter renewing an existing POA has to reissue it with the same specificity, not extend the old one informally.

Issuing one correctly is a short procedure, covered next, and the same specificity is what a counterparty checks when they ask for evidence of authority.
