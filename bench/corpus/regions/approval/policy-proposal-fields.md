# What a policy proposal states

A policy proposal is a drafts-and-proposals record, kept five years like any other draft, and it asks for more than the change itself.

## Required fields

| Field | What it captures |
|---|---|
| Subject touched | What the policy governs — this decides routing, not the size of the draft |
| What it replaces | The prior rule or practice, named directly, not implied |
| Effective date | When the change starts to apply once published |
| Authority | The role whose approval the subject requires |
| Affected teams | Who must consent before it reaches the approver |

## The trap

**Leaving "what it replaces" blank is the most common gap.** A proposal that changes how vendors are selected but never names the practice it overrides gets filed as a fresh rule sitting alongside the old one — both technically in force, and nobody notices until the two produce different answers on the same purchase.

Once these fields are filled in, the proposal enters review, where affected teams have a fixed window to respond.
