# Personal data

Recruitment files, payroll records, and vendor contact lists all involve personal data governed by the Personal Information Protection Act, not just by the company's own retention schedule. This sits alongside the draft and approval rules already in place, but personal data carries obligations that a signed-off draft does not satisfy on its own.

## What is covered

- What fields may be collected, and on what basis
- When consent has to be obtained, and how it must be recorded
- How to respond when someone asks what the company holds on them
- What has to happen if data is lost, leaked, or accessed without authorisation

## Where the rules live

Collection limits and consent requirements sit with whichever team gathers the data — usually HR or recruiting. A subject access request and a breach are both routed through the records desk, because both need a record independent of the team that caused the problem.

**A completed and approved draft does not by itself satisfy a personal data obligation.** A hire draft signed by the CEO settles who may be hired; it says nothing about what may be collected from the candidates who were not.

Retention still applies underneath all of this: an unsuccessful candidate's file follows the same six-month limit as any other unsuccessful application, personal data or not.
