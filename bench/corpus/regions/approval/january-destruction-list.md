# Building the January destruction list

Once a year, the records desk assembles the list of documents whose retention period matured in the previous calendar year and routes it for destruction.

## The steps
1. Pull every document whose retention date fell before 1 January of the current year, sorted by kind — contracts, tax invoices, drafts, general records, and the rest.
2. Cross-check the list against any open audit holds and remove anything still flagged.
3. Route the remaining list for sign-off before destruction proceeds.
4. Destroy the approved items and log each one.

## Why January, specifically
Retention periods are set in whole years, so a document maturing in any month of the prior year is swept up on the same annual cycle rather than tracked to the day. A tax invoice that cleared five years in April is destroyed alongside one that cleared in November.

## The trap
**Nothing on the list is destroyed until the hold check runs.** Skipping straight from "retention date has passed" to destruction is how documents under audit end up gone before the audit that needed them has closed.

What happens when something is destroyed outside this cycle, ahead of its own list, is where the real exposure shows up.
