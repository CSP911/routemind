# Document retention

How long each kind of document is kept, and what happens when it has run. The schedule itself is in
the table below this page — this is how the clock works, because that is the part that is got wrong.

## When the clock starts
**At the end of the business, not the date on the document.** A three-year contract signed in 2024 and
running to 2027 starts its ten years in 2027, not 2024. A tax invoice starts at the close of the
fiscal year it belongs to. Counting from the written date is the single most common retention error,
and it always destroys things early.

## Where the period comes from
Some of it is law — the Commercial Act for contracts, the Framework Act on National Taxes for books
and invoices, the Labor Standards Act for pay records. Some is this company's own decision, which is
usually longer than it has to be rather than shorter. The table says which is which, because a legal
period cannot be shortened by a decision here and a company period can.

## The one that runs the other way
**Unsuccessful job applications are kept six months and then destroyed.** Keeping them longer is a
breach, not caution. This is the only entry where holding on to something is the mistake.

## Destruction
Every January the records desk lists what has passed. The owning team checks the list before anything
is destroyed — **anything under audit or litigation stays, whatever its period says.** What is
destroyed goes in the log and is shredded. Nothing inside its period is destroyed at all, and nothing
destroyed can be recovered.
