# Responding to a counterparty's request for evidence of authority

## What to send
1. A seal certificate confirming the impression on the document matches the registered seal being used.
2. A copy of the power of attorney, if the signer is acting as an agent rather than the authority holder themselves.
3. A copy of the business registration certificate, if requested — doc admin holds the master copy.

## What stays in the building
**The original seal certificate and the original power of attorney are never handed to a counterparty — only certified copies leave.** Purpose-limited seal certificates exist for exactly this: they confirm a single transaction without exposing the registered seal to reuse elsewhere.

## Who authorises the release
Doc admin issues the copies; a division head or the CEO, matching whoever holds the underlying authority, confirms the request is legitimate before anything goes out. A request arriving by email from an unfamiliar contact is verified by phone before doc admin releases anything.

A counterparty who receives none of this and proceeds anyway on trust is taking on the company's own risk, not reducing it — which is why the previous document sets out what a power of attorney has to state before it is worth producing.
