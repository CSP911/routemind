# The auditor who asked for the original

An external auditor reviewing the year's contracts asked for the original of a 4,000,000 KRW service agreement to check the seal impression against the signing date. The division managing the account sent it straight from the file, reasoning that a seal is exactly what an auditor is meant to verify.

The original is meant to stay in custody once filed — that is what the seal certificate exists for. **A seal certificate confirms the impression without the original ever leaving the room**, and it is what should have gone out instead. Once the physical document left the building, the file no longer matched its own retention record until the contract was returned and re-logged, and for the two weeks it was out, there was no proof the seal on the copy still in the file was the one actually used.

The document came back, but the incident is why any request for an original — audit or otherwise — is answered with a certificate or a witnessed copy first, and the original moves only when neither will do.
