# An applicant list sent to the wrong address

A recruiting coordinator meant to send a shortlist to the division head and instead sent it to an external address one character off from the intended one. The spreadsheet held names, phone numbers, and interview scores for eleven candidates, four of whom had already been told they were unsuccessful.

The coordinator noticed within the hour, asked the external recipient to delete it, and considered the matter closed once that was confirmed. Nothing was escalated to the records desk.

**A confirmed deletion by the recipient does not close a breach — it does not change what has to be reported, or when.** The data had already left the company's control, which is what makes it a breach regardless of what happens to the copy afterward. The 24-hour escalation window ran from the moment the coordinator confirmed the send, not from any later confirmation that the copy was deleted.

The breach surfaced three weeks later when one of the unsuccessful candidates filed a subject access request, and the search turned up the mis-sent email in a way the coordinator's private apology had not covered.

Unsuccessful candidates' data should not have been sitting in an active spreadsheet three weeks on regardless of the breach — six months is the outer limit, not a target.
