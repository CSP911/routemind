# What may be collected

A collection form has to name the purpose before it names the fields. Collecting a home address for payroll is normal; collecting it on a purchase draft with no HR purpose is not, even where the field already exists on a template.

## Categories and basis

| Data category | Typical purpose | Basis needed |
|---|---|---|
| Name, contact details | Recruitment, payroll | Necessary for the process |
| Bank account | Salary payment | Necessary for the process |
| Resident registration number | Statutory filings only | Specific legal requirement |
| Health information | Insurance enrolment | Explicit consent |
| Family details | None by default | Explicit consent, stated purpose |

**The resident registration number is the field most often collected out of habit.** It may only be collected where a specific law requires it — tax and social insurance filings, mainly — never as a general identifier on an HR form. A form that asks for it "just in case" is itself a violation regardless of whether the number is ever used.

A field collected without a stated purpose cannot be salvaged later by writing one in afterward; the collection itself is the violation.

This sits upstream of consent: a field that fails the purpose test never reaches the question of whether consent would have covered it.
