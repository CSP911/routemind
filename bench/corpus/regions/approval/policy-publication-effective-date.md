# When a published policy takes effect

Publishing a policy is a separate act from writing it or getting consent — the policy takes effect on the date stated at publication, not the date it was signed off in review.

## What sets the date

| Event | What it does |
|---|---|
| Consent recorded | Clears the proposal for the approver, changes nothing yet |
| Approver signs | Confirms the policy is ready, still not live |
| Publication | Fixes the effective date and starts the policy's retention clock |

## The trap

**A policy is not retroactive to drafts already underway on its effective date.** A purchase drafted the day before a new spend rule is published is judged against the rule that applied when it was drafted, not the one that starts the next morning — the effective date on the policy is where it begins to govern, not a date it reaches backward to.

Whether the old policy needs to be formally closed out at that point is a matter for withdrawal, not publication.
