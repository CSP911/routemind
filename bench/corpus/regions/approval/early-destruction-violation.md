# A document destroyed before its schedule

A batch of general business records from 2022 is cleared out in a routine office move. Among them is a signed vendor comparison memo filed as a "general business record" — three-year retention — when it was in fact a drafting file entitled to the five-year period for drafts and proposals. It is destroyed after two years, one year short of even its intended period, and three short of the one it actually needed.

## Why the label mattered
Destruction lists are built from the kind a document was filed under, not from what it actually is. A drafting file mislabelled as a general record is swept into the shorter cycle and gone before anyone checks it against the correct table.

## What this exposes
If that memo is later needed — for a dispute, an audit, or simply to show how a decision was made — there is no record to produce, and no way to show retroactively that it was misfiled rather than correctly destroyed early.

## The trap
**A destruction list is only as accurate as the kind label on each file.** Fixing this after the fact is not possible; the only real defence is catching the mislabelling before the file is ever added to a January list.

The kind label is set once, at drafting — which is also where the correct retention period gets locked in.
