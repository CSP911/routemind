# Draft documents

A draft is how a decision is proposed, recorded and found again. One template for all of them, so
that a purchase, a hire and a contract all look the same to the person approving and to whoever reads
it years later. The fields themselves are on the form page below.

## When you need one
- Anything that spends money, whatever the amount.
- Anything that commits the company to an outside party.
- Anything that changes how a team works, or who does what.
- Anything done urgently on a verbal approval — filed **within three days**, after the fact.

## When you do not
Routine work already covered by a standing decision. If a draft last year approved a monthly service
at a fixed rate, the monthly payment is not a fresh draft — it is the existing authority being used.
Write that decision's number in the Authority field of whatever does come up.

## Writing one people will approve
- **Say the outcome, not the backstory.** Two or three lines. The person approving wants to know what
  will be true afterwards.
- **Name the authority.** The clause, or the number of the decision this follows from. If there is
  genuinely none, write "new" — a blank here is the most common reason a draft is sent back.
- **Attach before you route.** Quotes, the draft contract, the evidence. A chain that stalls while
  somebody hunts for an attachment is a chain everyone stops trusting.

The title carries a category tag in square brackets. It is the only thing that makes a document
findable later, and it is checked.
