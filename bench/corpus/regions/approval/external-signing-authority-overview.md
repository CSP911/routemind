# Signing authority for outside parties

## Who binds the company
A contract with an outside party is always signed by the CEO, regardless of value — the same rule that applies to any contract drafted internally. A purchase order under 3,000,000 KRW net of VAT can be signed by a division head, and one under 500,000 KRW by a team lead, but once the counterparty is external, the signer's authority has to be provable to someone who has no access to the approval chain.

## Why the chain doesn't travel
An outside party cannot see the drafting file, the consent record, or who approved what internally. **A signature that is perfectly valid inside the company means nothing to a counterparty unless it comes with separate evidence — a seal certificate, a power of attorney, or both.** Treating the internal approval as sufficient proof is the mistake that holds up closings.

## What evidence looks like
The two instruments that travel outside the company are the seal certificate, confirming an impression matches a registered seal, and a power of attorney, confirming someone may sign on another's behalf for a stated transaction. Neither substitutes for the other.
