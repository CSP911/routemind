# An urgent draft filed after the fact

Sometimes a decision cannot wait for the chain — a vendor payment has to clear same-day, or a piece of equipment fails and a replacement has to ship. The draft gets written and filed afterwards, marked as after-the-fact, rather than before.

## What still applies

- The same authority band based on the amount, net of VAT
- The same attachments the draft would have needed in advance
- A note of why it could not wait

## Example

A server component fails on a Friday. A team lead authorises a 2,800,000 KRW replacement to avoid a weekend outage and files the draft the following Monday, marked after-the-fact, routed to the division head as the amount requires.

## The trap

Filing late does not lower the bar. **An urgent purchase at 3,200,000 KRW still needs the CEO's approval — it is filed after the fact, not approved after the fact at a lower authority.** Treating urgency as a shortcut past the chain is the mistake this kind of draft exists to catch.

There is a window for how late "after the fact" is allowed to be — covered next.
