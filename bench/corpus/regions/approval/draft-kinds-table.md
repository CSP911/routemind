# Draft kinds at a glance

Six kinds of draft cover most of what gets filed. They differ in what decides the approver — amount, subject, or timing.

## Comparison

| Kind | Approver decided by | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase | Amount, net of VAT | 5 years (drafts) |
| Hire / dismissal | Subject — always CEO | 3 years (pay/employment) |
| Contract | Subject — always CEO | 10 years |
| Policy change | What it touches | 5 years (drafts) |
| After-the-fact | Same as the underlying action, filed within 5 business days | Same as the underlying action |
| Withdrawn and refiled | Treated as a new draft | Clock restarts on refiling |

## The trap

**Filing a draft under the wrong kind changes both its approver and its retention period silently.** A pay-related policy change filed as an ordinary policy draft looks routine and might clear a division head — but its subject puts it on the CEO's list, and its true retention is three years for pay matters, not the five years an ordinary draft gets.

Getting the kind right at the start is what makes the rest of the approval chain — who signs, what they consent to — apply correctly.
