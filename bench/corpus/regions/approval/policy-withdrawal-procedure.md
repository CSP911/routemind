# How a policy is withdrawn

A policy does not expire from disuse. It stays in force until someone with the authority that approved it files a withdrawal.

## Steps

1. Draft a withdrawal notice naming the policy and the reason.
2. Route it to the same authority level that originally published the policy — a division-head policy is withdrawn by a division head, a CEO-subject policy by the CEO.
3. Record the withdrawal date; the policy stops applying from that date forward, not retroactively.
4. File the notice as a drafts-and-proposals record, kept five years like the original proposal.
5. Notify the teams that had to consent to the original policy.

## The trap

**An unused policy is still a live policy.** A vendor-selection rule nobody has followed in two years still governs any purchase checked against it, and a division head cannot simply stop enforcing it — withdrawal has to be filed the same way the rule was published, or it remains the answer an audit will use.

Once withdrawn, responsibility for the gap it leaves falls to whoever owns that subject area next.
