# A chain with the wrong approver

A drafter puts together a 3,200,000 KRW purchase and routes it to the division head, because the division head has been closing out most of that team's spend for a year. The division head signs it. Spend of 3,000,000 KRW and over is the CEO's authority, not the division head's — regardless of how routine the purchase feels.

## Why the signature doesn't stand

Authority by amount is fixed by the spend table, not by who has usually handled a team's drafts. A division head signing above their band has not approved the draft in any way that counts; the draft is still open.

## What has to happen

The draft goes back through the chain to the CEO. The division head's signature is struck or superseded, not left alongside the CEO's as a second approval — there is one approver, and it has to be the one the amount requires.

**A wrong-approver signature is not a smaller error than a missing signature — an unauthorised sign-off has to be corrected before the draft can be treated as approved at all, the same as if no one had signed it.**

Whether a consenting team even had time to respond before a draft like this moved is the next thing worth checking.
