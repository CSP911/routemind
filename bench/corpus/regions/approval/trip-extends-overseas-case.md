# A domestic trip that grows an overseas leg

## What happened

A team lead planned a two-day trip from Seoul to Busan for a client visit and routed it to the division head, who approves domestic trips without issue. Before departure, the itinerary picked up a half-day stop in Fukuoka to meet a supplier on the way back. Nobody re-drafted the trip. The division head's sign-off was treated as covering the whole itinerary, since the overseas leg looked minor next to the domestic one.

## Why it didn't hold

**Adding an overseas leg to an already-approved domestic trip does not amend the existing approval — it requires a new draft routed to the CEO, because the itinerary was never eligible for division-head sign-off once it included a stop outside Korea.** The division head's authority is capped at domestic trips regardless of how short the overseas portion is or how little it costs. The Fukuoka stop was the whole trip, for approval purposes, not an addition to it.

## The fix

The trip was re-drafted from scratch with the full itinerary attached and routed to the CEO. The original division-head approval was closed out as withdrawn rather than amended, since it had authorised a trip that no longer existed once the itinerary changed.

The same destination test decides whether a request to work from abroad ever reaches the division head at all.
