# Reclassifying a draft filed under the wrong kind

A division head wanted to change how the team selects vendors — a standing rule, not a single spend — but filed it as a 2,400,000 KRW purchase so it would clear at division-head level instead of going to the CEO as a policy change. The amount was real; the framing was not.

The reviewer caught it because the draft's own purpose line described a rule that would apply to every future purchase, not a one-time cost. **A policy change routes by what it touches, and reframing a recurring rule as a one-off spend does not change what it touches** — the amount on the form is irrelevant once the actual subject is a standing policy.

| What was filed | What it actually was | Correct routing |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase, 2,400,000 KRW | Ongoing vendor-selection rule | Policy change — CEO |

The draft was withdrawn and refiled under the correct kind. Because a refiled draft starts its own chain and its own clock, the division head lost the time already spent waiting on the first approval — there is no credit for the wrong kind's progress.

Check the routing table for each kind before drafting, not after a reviewer sends it back.
