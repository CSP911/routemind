# Who signs off on destroying each kind

The January destruction list needs a sign-off before anything on it is actually destroyed, and who gives it scales with how sensitive the kind is — not with how large any one document happens to be.

| Document kind | Destruction sign-off |
|---|---|
| Contracts | CEO |
| Tax invoices and books | Division head |
| Pay and employment contracts | Division head |
| Social insurance filings | Division head |
| Drafts and proposals | Division head |
| General business records | Team lead |
| Unsuccessful applications | Records desk, no separate sign-off |

## Why contracts sit apart
Contracts are signed by the CEO regardless of value, so the same authority that put a contract into force is the one that clears it out of the file ten years later.

## The trap
**This is not the spend-authority table.** A 1,200,000 KRW service contract routes to the division head for approval when it is drafted, but its destruction, ten years on, still needs the CEO — the two decisions run on different rules, and it is easy to assume they track each other.

Unsuccessful applications are the one kind here where sign-off barely matters, because their deadline runs far shorter than any of the rest.
