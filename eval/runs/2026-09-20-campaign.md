Four arms, named for what they are. `rag` is fusion retrieval. `rag+rerank` adds the step a
retrieval engineer adds. `routing` is walk-and-read with no ranking anywhere. `routing+overlay`
adds the working set. The `+` column is **not one variable on both sides**: the reranker is what
you bolt onto retrieval, the working set is what RouteMind has. They are not each other's control.

## hit@10 (retrieval) · hit (routing)

**This table is the n=50 snapshot, and it stays.** A full 700-question routing census is running
after it. When that lands it goes in as a *separate* record with its own heading — it does not
overwrite this one. Two reasons: this is what was actually measured on 2026-09-20 with a stratified
sample of 50, and a later run at a different n is a different measurement, not a correction of this
one. If the census disagrees with the sample, the disagreement is itself the finding and both
numbers have to be visible for anyone to see it.

The retrieval and routing columns are **not the same measurement** and the `n` is there to say so.
`hit@10` asks whether the gold document is in the top ten of a ranked list. Routing `hit` asks
whether the walk named it as the source it answered from. The overall rows are not comparable to
each other either — 700 questions against 50 — and a routing figure is never to be quoted beside a
retrieval figure without both.

## What the numbers say, and what they do not

**Where RAG collapses, the walk does not.** `indirect` — a question in a person's words against rows
indexed by codes — is 0.028 for fusion and 0.069 with the reranker, on a census of 320. Both routing
arms are 50/50 across the whole frame. That is the headline and it is the thing the corpus was built
to produce.

**The reranker cannot rescue reachability.** It reorders candidates; it cannot add one. `recall@20`
is therefore a mathematical ceiling on `hit@10`, verified equal to two decimals on three corpora.
+0.025 overall is what reordering buys when the answer is usually not in the pool.

**Staleness splits by how far back you reach.** `changed` is 1.000 for plain fusion — a question
about the *current* rule finds the current page, which is what the retriever is good at. `stale-old`
is 0.133: reaching for the version two revisions back is where retrieval breaks worst, because every
newer version of the same subject outranks the one you want and they all look alike.

**The working set bought nothing here, and cost 60% more calls.**

|---|---|---|---|

| `boundary` | 5.2 | 11.5 | ×2.19 |

| **overall** | **7.1** | **11.4** | **×1.60** |

Same hit rate, ×1.60 the calls. State this plainly rather than around it: **on this frame the
overlay is a cost with no measured benefit.** The honest qualifier is that the frame has a ceiling —
`routing` is already 1.00 everywhere, so there is no headroom in which an improvement could show.
A working set is meant to stop an agent re-deciding something it has already decided, and these
walks are short enough that there is little to re-decide. The finding is *"not demonstrated on
questions this size"*, not *"does not work"*; the test that would separate those is a frame where
`routing` alone is below 1.00 — deeper trees, more areas, or questions needing five or six documents.

## What is not in these numbers

- **The reranker has been run once.** No variance estimate. Every `rag+rerank` figure is one draw.
- **Routing is n=50 against a census of 700.** An interval on 1.00 from 50 trials still admits a
  true rate near 0.93; "no miss in fifty walks" is what was observed, not "never misses".
- **One model per arm.** No Opus 5 re-run of anything a conclusion rests on.
- **Cost is counted in calls, not in the work of writing the table.** The map is a thing somebody
  maintains, and none of that labour appears in any column here.
- **Three corpus defects are carried deliberately** — see `eval/FREEZE.md`. Two cost calls rather
  than hits; the third (`B1`–`B4` against `A`/`B`/`C` with no cross-walk) makes one question shape
  genuinely unanswerable, and "not found" is the correct outcome for it.
- **One key defect was fixed mid-campaign** and both arms rescored from their kept reports — the
  `leave-accrual` / `accrual-rule` pair, written out in `eval/gold/AUDIT.md`. The fix was applied to
  all six questions keyed on that document, not to the three that tripped on it.

## Reproducing

```sh
./bench/crowd.py write                 # the extension, and its .fingerprint
./bench/serve.py                       # merge, derive, stamp, serve on 8101, check overlays
BENCH_EXTRA_CORPUS=bench/corpus-hard BENCH_USE_WHEN=maintained ./bench/mapcheck.py
./bench/run.py --gold eval/gold/hard.yaml          # rag, rag+rerank — census
./bench/walkprompt.py <qid> [--overlay]            # the prompt, generated, never hand-written
./bench/walkscore.py record --id <qid> --arm <arm> < /tmp/walk-<qid>[-overlay].md
./bench/walkscore.py report · rescore
```

`rmcli.py` refuses to run when `bench/corpus-hard/.fingerprint` and `data/bench-repo/FINGERPRINT`
disagree. That guard exists because the two once disagreed for an hour and nine walks were voided.
