# What routing costs that retrieval does not

Every accuracy number in this study favours routing. This page is the other column. It is measured
on the same corpus and the same questions, and it is the reason "routing wins" is not the whole
sentence.

    embeddings text-embedding-3-large, cached on disk
    call counts: all 700 walks · dollars and latency: 5 walks, measured with --output-format json

## Per question, what each arm actually does

| | `rag` | `rag+rerank` | `routing` |
|---|---|---|---|
| network round trips | **1** | **1.56** | **6.7** |
| what they are | one query embedding | + 0.56 reranking calls | 3.4 table opens, 3.3 document reads |
| new content processed | ~30 tokens | ~2,500 tokens | **14k–26k tokens** |
| wall clock | under a second | a few seconds | **23–111 s** |
| measured cost | negligible | see below | **$0.12–$0.28** |

`rag+rerank` averages 0.56 reranking calls rather than 1.0 because a question whose answer is not
among the fused candidates is not sent to the reranker — the miss is already decided. 390 of 700.

## The cost scales with how hard the question is

| lever | new content | output | cost | time | turns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `direct` | 16,842 | 1,330 | $0.122 | 23 s | 6 |
| `changed` | 13,799 | 1,927 | $0.128 | 24 s | 8 |
| `indirect` | 18,190 | 2,363 | $0.161 | 43 s | 9 |
| `indirect` | 18,591 | 2,286 | $0.170 | 47 s | 10 |
| `both` | 25,633 | 7,694 | $0.281 | 111 s | 14 |

**n=5.** Five walks, one each, chosen to span the levers. The median is $0.161 and the spread is
better read as a shape than a number: a question needing two documents costs roughly twice one
needing a single row, and takes five times as long.

**A note on the token figures.** The raw usage numbers look much worse than this — 200k to 500k
input tokens per walk — because every tool call resends the conversation so far. Almost all of it is
cache reads, billed at a fraction of fresh input; the genuinely new content is the 14k–26k above.
Quoting the 500k would overstate the cost by an order of magnitude, and quoting the ~20 uncached
input tokens would understate it by three. The dollar figure is the one that settles it, and it is
measured rather than derived.

## So what is actually being traded

**Latency is the one users feel.** Sub-second against 23–111 seconds is not a tuning difference; it
is a different interaction. Retrieval answers inside a request. A walk is a job you wait for, and at
the `both` lever it is nearly two minutes.

**Cost is roughly one to two orders of magnitude.** A reranking call moves ~2,500 tokens once; a
walk moves 14k–26k of new content across six to fourteen turns. The exact multiple depends on which
models you price, which is why the table gives tokens and calls alongside the dollars.

**Routing needs a table, and somebody writes it.** None of the numbers above include that. The map
in this corpus is five area descriptions and a set of revision notices — small, but written by hand
and *kept* by hand. `mapcheck` exists because it drifts, and this study lost three runs to a map
that had drifted without anyone noticing.

**And a wrong table is worse than no table.** One sentence in one forwarding note produced the only
miss in 700, and it produced a confident answer from the wrong era with a source attached. Retrieval
cannot fail that way, because it reads no map. See `eval/runs/2026-09-21-r6-fix.md`.

## The honest shape of the result

On this corpus, at the `indirect` lever, retrieval scores 0.028 and routing scores 1.000 over 320
questions each. That gap is large enough that the cost above is worth paying for those questions.

It is not worth paying for `direct` questions, where retrieval scores 0.991 and a reranker takes it
to 1.000 for one cheap call. Nothing in this study suggests walking a tree to answer a question
whose answer a retriever already finds first time — and 320 of these 700 questions are that kind.

The useful reading is not "routing beats RAG". It is: **retrieval collapses on a specific, nameable
class of question — one written in a person's words against documents indexed by codes, or asking
about a version that has since been replaced — and on that class it does not degrade, it fails
outright. Walking costs 20–100× more per question and fixes exactly that class.** Which arm to use
is a question about the mix of questions you actually get, and nothing here measures that mix for
anyone but this corpus.
