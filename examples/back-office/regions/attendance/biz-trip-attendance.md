# Attendance on a business trip

A trip is attendance before it is money. Getting the attendance right is what makes the expense claim
possible — the rule page below has the exact counting, and the handover page has the order to do it in.

## The dependency nobody expects
**The daily allowance is calculated from confirmed attendance days.** A day that is not in attendance
pays nothing, however many receipts you have for it. This is why a claim filed before attendance is
confirmed comes back, and why the two pages below are in this order.

## Before you go
File the trip request and get it approved. This is the step that matters most, because a trip taken
without one leaves those days blank in attendance — and blank is absence, not a trip. It is also the
only chance to get a weekend trip approved as time off in lieu.

## While you are away
Keep the local card slips. For an overseas trip, note the date whose exchange rate you intend to use;
picking it afterwards to suit the numbers is the kind of thing an audit asks about.

## When you get back
Confirm the dates in the attendance system **within three days**, then file the claim. The HR desk
checks the dates; the office & purchasing desk checks the amounts. They are different desks and they
ask different questions, so an answer from one is not an answer from the other.

## The cases that come up every year
- A trip that runs over a weekend — one day off in lieu, approved **in advance**, not claimed after.
- An overseas trip where departure and arrival overlap in local time — record local dates.
- A trip cancelled after approval — withdraw the trip request, or those days sit in attendance as a
  trip that never happened.
