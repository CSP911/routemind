# Annual leave

Two separate questions that people run together: **how many days you are entitled to**, which is a
calculation, and **how you take them**, which is a procedure. Both have their own page below.

## Entitlement, in one line
Fifteen days once you have a year of service, one day per completed month before that, and an extra
day every two years from your third — capped at twenty-five. Joining part-way through the year gives
a prorated first grant, which is the case people most often get wrong.

## Booking, in one line
In the attendance system, approved by your team lead, and three or more consecutive days also go to
the division head. It is leave when it is approved, not when it is agreed.

## The things that surprise people
- **Days are granted on 1 January, not on your anniversary.** Your service length decides how many;
  the calendar decides when they arrive.
- **They lapse.** Unused days do not roll over. The company pays out the lapsed balance, so nothing is
  lost in money, but a year with ten unused days is a year you worked more than you had to.
- **The first year works differently from every year after it** — monthly accrual, then replaced by
  the prorated grant. The two are not added together.
- **Leave during a leave of absence is reduced in proportion to days actually worked**, which is why
  someone returning from parental leave has fewer days than they expect.

## If you are planning a long absence
Anything of three days or more needs the division head as well, and the team has to be able to cover
it. Book it early — not for the approval, which is usually routine, but because the cover is not.
