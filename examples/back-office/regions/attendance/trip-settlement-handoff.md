# From trip to settlement

The point where this area hands over to Expenses. Two desks, two systems, and an order that cannot be
swapped — the numbered steps are on the page below.

## Why there is a handover at all
Attendance decides **how many days count**. Expenses decides **what those days pay**. Neither desk
will do the other's part, and the allowance is computed from the attendance figure, so attendance has
to be settled first. Filing the claim first does not speed anything up; it produces a claim with
nothing to check it against, which comes back.

## What travels across
| From attendance | Used by expenses for |
|---|---|
| Confirmed trip dates | The number of allowance days |
| Departure and arrival days | Whether the first and last days pay in full |
| Weekend or holiday days | Whether a day off in lieu was approved, not an extra payment |
| The trip request number | Tying the claim to an authorised trip |

## The three-day window
Confirm attendance within three days of returning. It is not a hard deadline in the system, but the
attendance month closes on the 3rd, and a trip that spans a month boundary and is confirmed late ends
up split across two pay runs — with the allowance landing in the later one.

## When it goes wrong
Almost always one of three things: the trip dates do not match what was approved, there is no
qualifying evidence for a spend above the threshold, or the allowance came through taxed because it
was over the tax-free cap. Each has its own page, and the expense side will name which one.
