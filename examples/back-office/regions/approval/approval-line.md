# Approval chain

The chain is the list of people a draft passes through before it is a decision. It has four kinds of
step and they do different things — the exact roles are in the role table below this page.

## Building one
1. **Start from the authority, not from the org chart.** Look up who may approve this amount or this
   subject, and that person is the last step. Adding their manager afterwards does not make the
   decision safer; it makes it late.
2. **Add the reviewer.** Normally your team lead. They are answerable for the content being right,
   not for the decision.
3. **Add every team that has to consent.** Money out, anything about people, an outside contract,
   anything touching personal data. A consenting team can stop it.
4. **Add readers last.** They should know; they cannot object.

## What goes wrong
- **A missing consent.** Approved and still not carried out. This is the single most common reason a
  draft is refiled.
- **An extra step added out of habit.** Once the delegated authority has approved, it is finished.
  Sending it further up is not caution, it is a breach of the delegation in the other direction.
- **The approver is away.** Their standing deputy approves. It does **not** move up a level.

## Urgent work
Something genuinely urgent may go ahead on a verbal approval, and the draft is filed within three
days to record it. With no draft afterwards the cost is personal. This is a narrow door — it exists
for an outage at midnight, not for a deadline somebody forgot.
