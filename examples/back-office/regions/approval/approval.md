# Documents & Approval

Anything this company decides is decided in a draft document. A verbal agreement in a meeting is not
a decision — it becomes one when it is drafted, approved by whoever has the authority, and filed.

## The four things people come here for
1. **Writing it.** Every draft goes on the same template, and the title carries a category tag so it
   can be found again. → Draft documents
2. **Who signs.** A chain is a drafter, a reviewer, an approver and — the part most often missed —
   the teams whose consent is required. → Approval chain
3. **Whether it has to go to the CEO.** Most of it does not. Authority is delegated by amount and by
   subject, and routing around that is as wrong as exceeding it. → Approval chain > Delegated authority
4. **How long to keep it.** Between six months and ten years depending on what it is, counted from
   when the business ended rather than when it was written. → Document retention

## What decides how far up it goes
Two things, and they are separate. **Subject** — contracts, hiring, anything with the corporate seal
go to the CEO whatever the amount. **Amount** — net of VAT, banded, and a purchase may not be split
to land in a lower band.

## The mistake that costs the most time
Filing without a required consent. The draft is approved, everyone believes it is settled, and the
work does not happen because accounting or HR never agreed to it. Check the consent list before you
route it, not after it comes back.
