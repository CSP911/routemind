# Purchase request

How to ask for something to be bought. The numbered steps are on the page below, and the amount bands
are under that.

## Before you write anything
Answer three questions, because the request will be sent back without them. **What exactly**, with a
specification precise enough that two suppliers would quote the same thing. **How many.** **By when** —
a real date, because the desk batches orders and a vague date means the later batch.

## Quotes
How many depends on the band. They have to be for the same specification, or they are not comparable
and the approver cannot use them. A **sole quote needs a written reason** — a sole supplier, or
continuity with a system already in place — and with a good reason it can drop one approval step.

Getting quotes is not the same as agreeing a price. A quote is a supplier telling you what they would
charge; agreeing before approval is committing the company without authority, and it is the thing this
process exists to prevent.

## Filing it
As a draft, with the quotes attached and — for a supplier not used before — the vendor registration
form. The approval chain follows the amount band, and the band is on the **total net of VAT**, with
same-item orders to one vendor inside three months added together.

## After approval
**The desk places the order.** You do not. Goods are checked in on arrival, and the tax invoice goes to
accounting after that check, not before — an invoice paid for goods nobody confirmed arriving is the
failure this sequence is arranged to avoid.

## If it was already bought
Say so immediately rather than filing a request that pretends otherwise. It can sometimes be
regularised. Filed as though the order had not happened, it cannot.
