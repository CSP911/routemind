# Office & purchasing desk

Takes purchase requests, registers vendors, issues and cancels corporate cards, and runs the office
itself. Contacts and cut-offs are on the page below.

## What it does that nobody else can
- **Places orders.** Teams do not. This is the single rule that keeps every invoice tied to an
  approved request and a registered supplier.
- **Registers vendors.** Two working days domestic, five overseas, and nothing can be ordered or paid
  before it is done.
- **Issues, stops and reissues corporate cards**, and decides temporary limit raises.
- **Decides travel amounts** — what a cap covers, and whether something above one was approved.

## The cut-offs are real
Purchase requests close **Tuesday and Thursday mornings**, same-day orders by 11:00. The desk batches
orders deliberately, so a request filed on Wednesday afternoon is a Thursday-morning order rather than
a Wednesday one. Knowing this is usually the difference between a delivery date that works and one
that does not — plan backwards from the cut-off, not from the day you need it.

## The conversation the desk has most often
Somebody has agreed a price with a supplier and comes to register them. The answer is that the process
starts again: quotes are part of the approval, and a price agreed before the approval is not a quote,
it is a commitment made without authority. It is worth knowing this before the phone call rather than
after.

## When something has gone wrong
For a lost card, stop it with the issuer first and tell the desk second. For anything already bought,
come immediately — the outcome is usually better than people fear, but only while it can still be
regularised.
