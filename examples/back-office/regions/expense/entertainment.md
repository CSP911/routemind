# Entertainment

Spending on customers and partners — meals, gifts, events. It has its own cap per head and its own
evidence rule, both on the page below, and they sit **on top of** the ordinary card limits rather than
replacing them.

## What makes something entertainment
Who is there. A meal with a customer or a partner is entertainment; the same meal with only our own
staff is a welfare cost and is recorded differently. This is not a technicality — the two are treated
differently for tax, which is why the attendee list matters so much.

## The rule that catches everyone
**Write down who attended and which company, on the receipt, at the time.** Entertainment with no
attendee list is disallowed in full — not reduced, disallowed. Reconstructing it a fortnight later
from a calendar is exactly the thing an audit asks about.

## What is not entertainment
- A working lunch with only colleagues → welfare.
- A conference or seminar fee → training.
- A gift to a public official → **not permitted at any amount.** This is not a cap question.
- Condolence and congratulation payments → their own line, with the invitation or notice as evidence.

## Practical order
1. Check the per-head cap before you book, not after.
2. Pay by card where you can — the slip is qualifying evidence.
3. Write the attendees on the slip immediately.
4. An event with no cap still needs **advance approval**; there is no version of this approved
   afterwards.

The per-transaction card cap still applies. Being entertainment does not lift it, and a client dinner
over your transaction cap needs a purchase request like anything else.
