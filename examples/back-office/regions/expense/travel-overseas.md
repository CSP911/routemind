# Overseas travel

Daily allowance and lodging by region band, the exchange rate rule, and which day's rate applies. The
bands are in the table below, and a worked settlement is in the case page under it.

## Three things that are different from a domestic trip
1. **Banded by region, in USD.** Where you went sets the rate, not how far it was.
2. **The departure day and the arrival day each count as a full day.** There is no half day at either
   end, even on an overnight flight.
3. **The exchange rate is fixed by the departure date**, not by the day you settle. This removes the
   temptation to pick a favourable rate afterwards, and it is checked — **the claim states the rate
   date, and one without it is sent back.**

## Card payments abroad
Taken at the amount the issuer billed in KRW. They are not re-converted at the departure rate, because
the issuer has already done the conversion and that figure is what left the company. Mixing the two
methods in one claim is fine as long as each line says which it is.

## Cash abroad
This is where trouble comes from. A cash spend over the equivalent of 30,000 KRW needs qualifying
evidence like any other, and a foreign simple receipt usually is not one. **Keep overseas cash spending
inside the daily allowance** — the allowance needs no evidence at all, so anything covered by it is
already settled.

## Tax
More of an overseas allowance sits above the tax-free cap than a domestic one, so a visible part of it
is taxed on the payslip. The claim is not reduced; the tax appears separately. → Tax-free allowances.
