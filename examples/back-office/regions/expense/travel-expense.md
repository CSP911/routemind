# Travel expenses

What the company pays for on a trip and what it does not. Domestic and overseas have separate caps —
both on the pages below — and they work differently enough that it is worth reading the right one.

## What is always paid
- **Fares**, at actual cost, within the class rules.
- **Lodging**, at actual cost up to the nightly cap, with a receipt.
- **A daily allowance**, paid without receipts, covering meals and local transport.

## What is not
Anything personal on the trip, an upgrade paid by choice, a companion's costs, and spending above a cap
that was not approved **before** it happened. Approval after the fact does not exist for travel — this
is stricter than the rest of Expenses and it is deliberate, because trips are planned.

## The dependency to remember
**The allowance is paid on confirmed attendance days.** Attendance is settled first, then the claim.
A day you were away but which is not in attendance pays nothing, and no amount of evidence changes
that — it is a different desk and a different question. → Attendance & Leave.

## Domestic and overseas differ in three ways
1. **Currency and rate.** Overseas is banded in USD and converted at the departure-day rate.
2. **The first and last days.** Overseas counts both departure and arrival as full days.
3. **Tax.** More of the overseas allowance sits above the tax-free cap, so more of it is taxed on the
   payslip. That is not a deduction from the claim; it is how the allowance rides on pay.
   → Tax-free allowances.

Using your own car is paid per kilometre rather than by fuel receipts, on either kind of trip.
