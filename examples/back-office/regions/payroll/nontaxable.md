# Tax-free allowances

The allowances no tax is charged on, and the cap on each. The table is on the page below; this is how
the caps behave, which is the part that produces surprises.

## Only the excess is taxed
Going over a cap does not cancel the exemption. A meal allowance of 250,000 KRW against a 200,000 KRW
cap taxes 50,000 KRW. People expect the whole line to become taxable and it does not — this is worth
knowing before querying a payslip.

## The conditions matter as much as the caps
Each allowance carries one, and failing it makes the whole line taxable rather than reducing it.
- **Meals** — only where meals in kind are not also provided. A canteen and the allowance together do
  not work.
- **Own-car use** — your own vehicle, used for work, and **not alongside a per-kilometre claim** for
  the same vehicle.
- **Childcare** — a child aged six or under. It stops in the year they turn seven, automatically.

## The trip allowance is the difficult one
It is tax free as "reasonable actual cost", and the policy rates are treated as reasonable. Two things
break that:
- **A day attendance has not confirmed.** With no attendance behind it the allowance is not a travel
  cost at all; it is pay, and fully taxable.
- **Claiming a meal receipt on a day you also drew the allowance.** The allowance already covers meals,
  so the receipt is double counting and that amount moves back into taxable pay.

Neither is a penalty. Both are the allowance being reclassified as what it actually was, and both are
avoidable at the point of claiming rather than at the point of being taxed.
