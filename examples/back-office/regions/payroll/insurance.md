# Social insurance

Pension, health, long-term care, employment and industrial accident. The rates and who pays what are
in the table below; this is why the number moves when nothing about your pay has changed.

## Four insurances, two payers
You and the company each pay roughly half of pension, health and long-term care. Employment insurance
is split unevenly, and **industrial accident is the company's alone** — it never appears as a
deduction, which is why the payslip shows four lines and not five.

## Why the deduction changes without a pay rise
- **April.** Health insurance is recalculated on last year's actual pay and the difference is
  collected or refunded through April's pay. A bonus last year shows up as a larger deduction this
  April, once.
- **July.** The pension's standard monthly income is reset. A rise is applied from then, not from when
  the pay rise was agreed.
- **A reported pay change.** Contributions follow the reported figure, and reporting lags the
  agreement. The catch-up arrives in one month rather than being spread.

None of these are errors, and all three are the usual reason a deduction jumps.

## During a leave of absence
Cover does not stop. **Health insurance payment can usually be deferred** and settled in instalments
after you return; pension contributions can be suspended, though that period then does not count
toward the contribution record — which matters for a pension years away and is worth a moment's
thought rather than a default.

The payroll desk files both, and it files them from the date the leave is confirmed. **Tell the desk
as soon as it is agreed**, not when the leave starts — a late filing is corrected retroactively, and
that correction lands as one large deduction.
